STEP 3 · TEST

Structure fits function

25 – 35 min · Apply
  • Cells of the same organism all carry the same DNA, but they do not all look the same. A cell builds more of whatever it uses most.
  • So the NUMBER of an organelle tells you about the cell's job, just as much as which organelles are present at all.
  • Always finish the chain: the cell does this job → so it needs a lot of this process → so it has many of this organelle.

In your book, then here

5(a). A muscle cell contains far more mitochondria than a skin cell. Explain why. [1]
Support hintJob → process → organelle.
5(b). A root hair cell is a plant cell, but it contains no chloroplasts. Explain why this makes sense. [1]
Support hintWhere is it, and what do chloroplasts need?
5(c). Suggest ONE structure you would expect to find in large numbers in a cell that secretes a lot of protein, and say why. [1]
Support hintWhere are proteins made?

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

What makes a question testable?

35 – 45 min · Criterion B
The question we are going to examine
"How does magnification affect our ability to observe cell structures?"
It sounds like a good research question. Read it again, slowly, and decide whether it really is.
  • A TESTABLE question names what you CHANGE (the independent variable) and what you MEASURE (the dependent variable).
  • The dependent variable must be something you can put a number on: counted, timed, or read off an instrument.
  • Usable measures here: the number of cells visible in the field of view, the number of named structures you can identify, or the width of one cell in µm.

In your book, then here

6(a). In this question, what exactly would you CHANGE, and what exactly would you MEASURE? [2]
Support hintThe change is fine. "Our ability to observe": can you put a number on it? Say WHY not.
6(b). State the independent variable, the dependent variable, and TWO controlled variables. [2]
Support hintIV and a countable DV. Two controls: "the same…" twice.
6(c). Rewrite the question so it names a measurable dependent variable. Start with "How does…". [1]
Support hintName something countable.

STEP 5 · CHALLENGE

Ethics, then design your own investigation

45 – 67 min · Criterion B

Ethics: collecting biological samples

The discussion question
Should scientists be allowed to collect biological samples from any organism, anywhere, for research? Plants and micro-organisms have been collected in one country, developed into medicines in another, and sold back at a price the first country could not afford. Human tissue has been used in research without the person ever being asked. There is no single correct answer: the quality of your reasoning is what counts.
Ground rules. We discuss arguments, never people. Every view is challenged politely and by reason. If you prefer not to speak, write your point on a slip

In your book, then here

7(b). Agree THREE principles for responsible biological investigation. Write them as instructions, not opinions. [3]
Support hintAsk whom? How much may you take? What do you owe the community?
