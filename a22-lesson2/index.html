Structures common to all cells

Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic cellsAmoeba Sisters · 5:28 · plays here
  • PLASMA MEMBRANE: a phospholipid bilayer that separates the cell from its surroundings and controls what enters and leaves.
  • CYTOPLASM: the watery contents of the cell (mostly water with dissolved substances) where most metabolic reactions happen, catalysed by enzymes.
  • DNA: the genetic material: genes code for the cell's proteins.
  • RIBOSOMES: make proteins by translating mRNA. Prokaryotes have 70S ribosomes; the cytoplasm of eukaryotes has larger 80S ribosomes.
  • Viruses have none of a cell's machinery except nucleic acid, which is why they are not cells.

STEP 3 · TEST

Inside a prokaryote

15 – 30 min · A2.2.5 · labelled diagram
  • CELL WALL: outside the membrane, made of peptidoglycan; keeps the shape and prevents the cell bursting by osmosis.
  • NUCLEOID: the region of cytoplasm containing the single, circular chromosome. There is no nucleus and no nuclear membrane.
  • NAKED DNA: prokaryotic DNA is not wrapped around histone proteins (eukaryotic DNA is).
  • PLASMIDS: small extra rings of DNA, often carrying genes such as antibiotic resistance; can be passed between bacteria.
  • 70S RIBOSOMES free in the cytoplasm. No membrane-bound organelles: no mitochondria, chloroplasts, ER or Golgi.
  • Some prokaryotes also have: a CAPSULE (slime layer for protection and sticking to surfaces), PILI (hair-like, for attachment and for transferring plasmids) and a FLAGELLUM (rotates to move the cell).
  • Size about 1–5 µm.
