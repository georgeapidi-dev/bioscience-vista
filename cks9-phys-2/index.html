STEP 1 · QUESTION

Question: heat or temperature?

0 – 10 min
  • Temperature tells you how hot or cold something is, measured in °C with a thermometer. It depends on the average movement (kinetic energy) of the particles.
  • Heat is energy transferred from a hotter place to a colder place, measured in joules (J).
  • The total thermal energy of an object depends on its temperature, its mass and its material.

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Explore: mass matters

10 – 22 min
  • Heating 1 kg of water from 20 °C to 30 °C needs about 42 000 J; heating 2 kg by the same amount needs twice as much.
  • Different materials need different amounts of energy: water needs more energy to warm up than the same mass of oil or metal.
  • When two objects at different temperatures touch, energy flows from hot to cold until they reach the same temperature.

STEP 3 · TEST

Test: energy is conserved

22 – 35 min
  • Energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be transferred from one store to another. This is the law of conservation of energy.
  • Energy stores include: kinetic, gravitational potential, chemical, thermal, elastic.
  • In any transfer: total energy in = useful energy out + wasted energy out.

Your answers

Q5. Calculate the wasted energy. [4]
Lamp: 100 J in, 80 J light out
Motor: 200 J in, 140 J kinetic out
Kettle: 1000 J in, 925 J heats the water
Car engine: 500 J in, 350 J kinetic out

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Explain: energy transfer diagrams

35 – 48 min
  • A Sankey diagram shows energy transfers with arrows whose width is proportional to the amount of energy.
  • The useful energy goes straight on; the wasted energy curves off (usually as heat).
  • Wasted energy spreads into the surroundings and becomes less useful: it is dissipated.
