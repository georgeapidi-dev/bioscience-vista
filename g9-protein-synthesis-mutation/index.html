Recap: how DNA copies itself

about 8 min · Criterion A

🎬 Videos for this lesson

▶ DNA Replication (Amoeba Sisters)Watch for: why is it called semi-conservative?
  • DNA is copied before a cell divides, so each new cell gets a full copy. This is REPLICATION.
  • The two strands UNZIP. Each old strand is used as a TEMPLATE to build a new strand.
  • Bases always pair the same way: A with T, and C with G.
  • Each new DNA molecule has ONE OLD strand and ONE NEW strand. This is called SEMI-CONSERVATIVE replication.

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

From gene to protein: transcription and translation

about 21 min · Criterion A

Part A · Transcription: DNA to mRNA

▶ From DNA to Protein – 3D (yourgenome)Watch the first half. Where is the mRNA made?
  • A GENE is a section of DNA that holds the instructions for one PROTEIN.
  • DNA is too big to leave the NUCLEUS. So the cell makes a small copy of one gene, called mRNA (messenger RNA).
  • Making the mRNA copy is called TRANSCRIPTION. It happens in the nucleus.
  • mRNA has no T. It uses U (URACIL) instead. So DNA A → mRNA U, T → A, C → G, G → C.
  • The mRNA then leaves the nucleus and goes to a RIBOSOME.

Part B · Translation: mRNA to protein

▶ Protein Synthesis: Transcription and Translation (Amoeba Sisters)Watch for: what is a codon, and what does the ribosome do?
  • TRANSLATION means using the mRNA to build a protein. It happens at a RIBOSOME in the cytoplasm.
  • The ribosome reads the mRNA THREE bases at a time. Each group of three is a CODON.
  • Each codon stands for one AMINO ACID. AUG means START. UAA means STOP.
  • The amino acids join in a chain. The chain folds up to make a PROTEIN.
  • To read a codon table: split the mRNA into threes, then look up each codon.

STEP 3 · TEST

Mutations, and why a frameshift does the most damage

about 17 min · Criterion A
▶ Mutations (Amoeba Sisters)Watch for: what are the different ways DNA can change?
  • A MUTATION is a change in the order of bases in DNA.
  • SUBSTITUTION: one base is swapped for another. INSERTION: an extra base is added.
  • DELETION: a base is removed. INVERSION: a section of bases is turned back to front.
  • Mutations can happen by mistake when DNA is copied, or be caused by MUTAGENS such as UV light, X-rays and chemicals in tobacco smoke.
  • Most mutations have no effect. Some are harmful. A few are helpful.

Frameshift

▶ Genetics in 60 seconds: Frameshift MutationWatch for: what happens to the codons after an extra base?
A sentence of three-letter words shows how a frameshift works
Normal: THE · CAT · ATE · THE · RAT
Insert an X: THE · XCA · TAT · ETH · ERA · T
Substitute one letter: THE · BAT · ATE · THE · RAT
After the insertion, every word is wrong. After the substitution, only one word changes.
  • The ribosome reads mRNA in groups of three, starting from the beginning. This is the READING FRAME.
  • An INSERTION or a DELETION moves the reading frame along. Every codon after the mutation changes. This is a FRAMESHIFT.
  • A frameshift changes many amino acids, so the protein usually cannot do its job.
  • A SUBSTITUTION changes only ONE codon, so at most one amino acid changes. Sometimes nothing changes at all.

Investigation: does UV light damage yeast?

about 18 min · Criteria B and C
▶ How UV Causes Cancer and AgingWatch for: how does UV light damage DNA? (Your teacher may play only part of it.)
  • UV light (in sunlight and UV lamps) is a MUTAGEN. It can damage DNA. In skin, this can lead to sunburn and skin cancer.
  • If UV damages too much of a yeast cell's DNA, the cell dies and cannot grow into a COLONY.
  • A fair test: the INDEPENDENT variable is what you change; the DEPENDENT variable is what you measure; CONTROL variables are kept the same.
  • A HYPOTHESIS is a prediction with a reason: "If …, then …, because …".

STEP 5 · CHALLENGE

Mutations and people: sickle cell disease

about 11 min · Criterion D
▶ Sickle Cell Disease ExplainedWatch for: what goes wrong in the red blood cells?
One substitution, one codon, one amino acid
NormalGAG
codes for glutamic acid
SickleGUG
codes for valine: the red blood cells become curved like a sickle
  • Sickle cell disease is caused by a SUBSTITUTION in the gene for HAEMOGLOBIN, the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen.
  • One codon changes from GAG to GUG, so one amino acid changes. The red blood cells become curved like a SICKLE.
  • Sickle cells can block blood vessels. This causes pain and tiredness, because less oxygen reaches the body.
  • It is common in parts of Africa, including Kenya. People who carry one copy of the gene are partly protected from MALARIA.
  • How science helps: testing babies at birth, medicines, blood transfusions, bone marrow transplants and new GENE THERAPY, which is very expensive.
