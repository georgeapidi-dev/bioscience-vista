By the end of this lesson I will be able to

  • Say whether a solid, a liquid and a gas keep their own shape.
  • Say which one can be squashed, and which one spreads.
  • Explain what I saw using the word particles.
The big ideaEverything is made of tiny particles. Solid, liquid and gas have the SAME particles: only how they are arranged and how they move changes.
SafetyNever point a syringe at anyone (there are no needles). Push with your thumb only. Wipe up spilt water at once. Do not taste anything, and keep food colouring off skin and clothes.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

What the particles were doing

45 – 55 min · Question 4
  • Everything is made of tiny PARTICLES, far too small to see. We draw them as small circles: this is a MODEL.
  • SOLID: particles packed tightly in neat rows. They only vibrate on the spot, so a solid keeps its own shape.
  • LIQUID: particles still touch, but are jumbled and can SLIDE past each other, so a liquid takes the shape of its container.
  • GAS: particles are very FAR APART with big gaps, and move quickly in all directions, so a gas spreads out and fills the room.
  • The particles are exactly the SAME in all three. Only how they are arranged and how they move changes. The particles do not get bigger in a gas: only the gaps do.
