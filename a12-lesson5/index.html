The Hershey and Chase experimentYourekaScience · 5:16 · plays here
  • Organism: bacteriophage T2, a virus made only of a protein coat and DNA, which infects E. coli.
  • Two batches of phages: one grown with PHOSPHORUS-32 (labels the DNA: P is in DNA, not in protein); one with SULFUR-35 (labels the protein: S is in the amino acids cysteine and methionine, not in DNA).
  • Each batch infected unlabelled bacteria. A BLENDER knocked the phage coats off the bacteria; a CENTRIFUGE spun the heavier bacteria into a PELLET, leaving the light phage coats in the SUPERNATANT.
  • The radioactivity of the pellet and supernatant was measured.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Chargaff's rules

35 – 55 min · A1.2.15
  • Erwin Chargaff analysed the base composition of DNA from many species (late 1940s).
  • CHARGAFF'S RULES (double-stranded DNA): the amount of adenine equals thymine (A = T) and guanine equals cytosine (G = C). So total purines = total pyrimidines (A + G = C + T).
  • The ratio of A+T to G+C VARIES between species. This disproved the earlier "tetranucleotide hypothesis" that DNA was a simple repeating unit of all four bases in equal amounts (too simple to carry information).
  • Chargaff's data helped Watson and Crick deduce complementary base pairing.
