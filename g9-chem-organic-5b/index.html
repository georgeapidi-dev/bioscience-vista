Guiding question: How does river water become safe to drink from a tap?

Water from a muddy river passes through a treatment works and comes out of a tap clear and safe. In dry coastal cities such as Mombasa, engineers are building desalination plants to turn sea water into drinking water. Each step removes a different problem, and each method has a cost.

By the end of this lesson I will be able to

  • Describe the stages of water treatment and the purpose of each.
  • Evaluate methods of producing drinking water, including desalination.
  • Screening: removes large debris (sticks, plastic).
  • Sedimentation: particles settle (aluminium sulfate can clump them first).
  • Filtration through sand and gravel: removes fine particles.
  • Chlorination (or UV, ozone): kills microbes.
  • Sometimes fluoride is added for teeth; pH is adjusted.
  • Distillation: boil sea water, condense the steam. Pure but huge energy cost.
  • Reverse osmosis: pump sea water at high pressure through a membrane that lets water through but not salts.
  • Problems: energy, cost, concentrated brine returned to the sea harms marine life.
The big ideaScreening, sedimentation, filtration and chlorination remove debris, solids and microbes; desalination by distillation or reverse osmosis makes sea water drinkable but uses a lot of energy.

✏️ Worked example: Energy for desalination
  1. Reverse osmosis: about 3.5 kWh per m³ of fresh water.
  2. A city of 1 million using 100 litres each per day needs 100 000 m³/day.
  3. Energy: 100 000 × 3.5 = 350 000 kWh per day.
  4. That is like running about 14 600 kW continuously: a power station's worth.
