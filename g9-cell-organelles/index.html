What is a eukaryotic cell?
A EUKARYOTIC cell keeps its DNA inside a NUCLEUS. Plants, animals, fungi and many other living things are made of eukaryotic cells. (Bacteria are not: their DNA floats free, so they are called prokaryotic.)
Inside a eukaryotic cell are ORGANELLES: small structures that each have their own job, in the same way that the rooms of a house each have their own job.

Fact File: the eight structures you need to know

StructureWhat it looks likeIts job in the cellFound in
NucleusLarge and round, with a membrane that has holes (pores) in itContains the DNA. Controls everything the cell does, by sending instructions out to the ribosomes.plant and animal
CytoplasmJelly-like liquid that fills the cellWhere the organelles sit and where most of the cell's chemical reactions happen.plant and animal
Cell membraneA very thin layer around the outside of the cellControls what enters and leaves. It is partially permeable: it lets some things through but not others.plant and animal
RibosomesTiny dots, far too small to see with a school microscopeMake proteins, following the instructions from the nucleus.plant and animal
MitochondrionOval, with a folded inner membraneCarries out respiration: releases energy from glucose so the cell can use it.plant and animal
ChloroplastOval and green, with stacks of green discsContains chlorophyll, which absorbs light. Carries out photosynthesis: makes glucose using light energy.plant only
Cell wallA thick, strong layer outside the membrane, made of celluloseSupports the cell, keeps its shape and stops it bursting. Fully permeable: everything passes through.plant only
VacuoleA large space filled with cell sap (water with sugars and salts)Stores water and dissolved substances, and pushes outwards to keep the cell firm.large and permanent in plants; small and temporary in animals
