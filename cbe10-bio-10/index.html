Chloroplasts and the light and dark stages

Guiding question: How do plants make food from light, water and carbon dioxide?

Every grain of maize in Kitale, every tea leaf in Kericho and every rice field in Vietnam is made mostly from carbon dioxide taken from the air. Plants trap the energy of sunlight to build sugar. Where in the leaf does this happen, and what are the light and dark stages?

STEP 1 · QUESTION

Question: where does a tree's mass come from?

0 – 10 min
  • Photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, using light energy absorbed by chlorophyll.
  • 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (light, chlorophyll).
  • Glucose is used in respiration, converted to starch for storage, cellulose for cell walls, sucrose for transport, and proteins and lipids.

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Explore: the chloroplast

10 – 22 min
  • Chloroplasts are found mainly in palisade mesophyll cells.
  • A double membrane surrounds the stroma (fluid with enzymes, starch grains and DNA).
  • Stacks of membranes called grana (made of thylakoids) hold chlorophyll: a large surface area to absorb light.

STEP 3 · TEST

Test: light and dark stages

22 – 35 min
  • Light stage (on the grana): chlorophyll absorbs light energy; this energy splits water (photolysis) into hydrogen ions and oxygen; energy is stored in ATP; oxygen is released as a by-product.
  • Dark stage (light-independent, in the stroma): carbon dioxide combines with a 5-carbon compound (carbon dioxide fixation); hydrogen from the light stage and energy from ATP reduce it to make glucose. It is controlled by enzymes, so it depends on temperature.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Explain: limiting factors

35 – 48 min
  • The rate of photosynthesis is limited by whichever factor is in shortest supply: light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration or temperature (and water, chlorophyll).
  • Investigation: count oxygen bubbles from pondweed (e.g. Elodea) at different distances from a lamp.
  • Greenhouse farmers in Kenya and the Netherlands raise yields by controlling light, temperature and CO₂.
