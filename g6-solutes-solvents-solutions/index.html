STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Solute + solvent = solution

8 – 18 min · Task 2 · Criterion A
  • The SOLUTE is the substance that dissolves (sugar).
  • The SOLVENT is the liquid that dissolves it (water).
  • Together they make a SOLUTION (sugar water): SOLUTE + SOLVENT = SOLUTION.
  • A solution is a homogeneous mixture. It is see-through, though it may have a colour, like tea or copper sulfate solution.
  • Water dissolves so many substances that it is called the "universal solvent".

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Dissolving, mass and strength

35 – 48 min · Task 4 · Criterion A
  • When a solute dissolves, its particles spread out between the solvent particles. It does not disappear.
  • MASS IS NOT LOST: 100 g water + 10 g sugar = 110 g sugar solution.
  • Dissolving is NOT melting. Melting needs heat and only one substance. Dissolving needs a solute AND a solvent.
  • A CONCENTRATED solution has a lot of solute; a DILUTE solution has only a little.
  • A SATURATED solution cannot dissolve any more solute: extra solid stays at the bottom.
  • Stirring, warmer water and smaller grains make a solute dissolve FASTER.
