STEP 2 · EXPLORE

The flowering plant life cycle

5 – 16 min · Transfer
  • A flowering plant runs the same cycle you met in humans: MEIOSIS halves the chromosome number to make haploid gametes; FERTILISATION puts the two halves together; MITOSIS builds the new plant.
  • The male gamete is inside a POLLEN GRAIN, made in the ANTHER. The female gamete is inside an OVULE, made in the OVARY. Both are haploid.
  • The flower is the organ that carries all of this. One flower usually has both male and female parts, which animals almost never do.
  • After fertilisation the OVULE becomes the SEED and the OVARY becomes the FRUIT.

STEP 3 · TEST

The parts of a flower and their jobs

16 – 34 min · Criterion A

🎬 Videos for this lesson

▶ Flower anatomy and pollination (Cognito)The parts of a flower, named and explained. Watch before the table.
  • The male part is the STAMEN = ANTHER + FILAMENT. The anther makes the pollen; the filament holds it up where an insect will brush past.
  • The female part is the CARPEL = STIGMA + STYLE + OVARY. The stigma catches pollen, the style is the road down to the ovary, and the ovary holds the OVULES.
  • SEPALS are the small green leaf-like parts that wrapped and protected the flower while it was a bud.
  • PETALS are large, bright and scented in insect-pollinated flowers: the advertisement. NECTARIES at the base make sugary nectar that pays the insect.
  • Nothing is decoration: every part exists to get pollen from an anther onto a stigma, and then one male gamete to one female gamete.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Pollination: self and cross

34 – 50 min · Criterion A
▶ Sexual reproduction in plantsPollination in full, in exam language. Watch, then answer Question 5.
  • POLLINATION is the TRANSFER of pollen from an anther to a stigma. That is the whole definition: no fusion has happened yet.
  • SELF-POLLINATION: pollen lands on the stigma of the same flower, or another flower on the SAME plant.
  • CROSS-POLLINATION: pollen is carried to the stigma of a flower on a DIFFERENT plant of the same species.
  • Cross-pollination mixes genes from two parents, so the offspring show more VARIATION, which lets a population survive a new disease or a changed climate.
  • Self-pollination is more reliable (no insect or wind needed; a lone plant can still reproduce), but the offspring are far more alike, so one disease can take out the lot.

STEP 5 · CHALLENGE

Insect-pollinated and wind-pollinated

50 – 66 min · Compare and explain
  • An insect must be ATTRACTED, then LOADED, then UNLOADED: the flower advertises (big scented petals), pays (nectar), and keeps its anthers and stigma INSIDE where the insect brushes past.
  • The wind cannot be attracted or aimed: the flower spends nothing on petals, scent or nectar, and hangs its anthers and stigma OUTSIDE in the moving air.
  • Insect pollen: large, sticky and spiky, so it clings. Wind pollen: small, smooth and very light, so it drifts.
  • Insect stigma: small and sticky. Wind stigma: large and feathery, to filter pollen out of the air.
  • Wind pollination is enormously wasteful, so the plant makes far more pollen. That waste is also why hay fever exists.
