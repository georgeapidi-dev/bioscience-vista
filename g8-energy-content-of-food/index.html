By the end of this lesson I will be able to

  • Explain how burning food transfers energy to water.
  • Measure a temperature rise safely and accurately.
  • Calculate the energy released in joules, and per gram of food.
  • Explain why the values are lower than the accepted values, and suggest improvements.
Safety firstTell your teacher now if you have ANY food allergy. Goggles on from the moment the Bunsen is lit. Never hold a burning sample in your fingers, and never pick up a hot needle.
The equationEnergy (J) = mass of water (g) × 4.2 × temperature rise (°C). 1 cm³ of water = 1 g.
TimeAbout 75 minutes. Show all three steps in every calculation and always write the unit.

Notes

Show every step. Energy (J) = mass of water × 4.2 × temperature rise. Energy per gram (J/g) = energy ÷ mass of food. Divide J/g by 1000 to get kJ/g. Accepted values: lipid about 37 kJ/g; carbohydrate and protein about 17 kJ/g.
