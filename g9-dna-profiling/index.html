STEP 1 · QUESTION

What is a DNA profile, and how do we compare them?

about 14 min · Criteria A and C

🎬 Videos for this lesson

▶ What is DNA fingerprinting?Watch for: why is everyone's band pattern different?
  • Everyone's DNA is a little bit different, except identical twins.
  • A DNA PROFILE is a pattern of bands made from a person's DNA. It is also called a DNA FINGERPRINT.
  • Each column is a LANE. One lane shows one person's DNA.
  • Each dark line is a BAND. A band is a group of DNA pieces that are all the same size.
  • Bands at the same height are DNA pieces of the same size.
  • Some parts of DNA repeat many times. The number of repeats is different for different people, so their bands are in different places.

Comparing profiles

▶ DNA fingerprinting in a real labWatch for: how do the students compare their DNA bands?
  • Lay a RULER across the page (or the screen).
  • Check ONE height at a time: does each lane have a band here?
  • If two lanes both have a band at the same height, those bands MATCH.
  • The more bands that match, the more alike the two profiles are.
  • EVIDENCE means saying exactly which bands line up, for example "the top two bands".

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

How a DNA profile is made

about 10 min · Criterion A
▶ Gel electrophoresis and DNA fingerprintingWatch for: what are the steps, and which way does the DNA move?
  • Step 1: take DNA from cells, for example blood, saliva or hair.
  • Step 2: CUT the DNA into pieces using ENZYMES (chemical scissors).
  • Step 3: put the pieces into WELLS in a GEL and switch on the electricity. This is GEL ELECTROPHORESIS. Its job is to sort the DNA pieces by SIZE.
  • Step 4: add a STAIN so the bands can be seen, then compare the patterns.
  • DNA is NEGATIVE (−) because of the PHOSPHATE in its backbone. Opposites attract, so DNA moves towards the POSITIVE (+) end.
  • Uses: catching criminals, paternity tests (finding a child's father) and identifying bodies.

STEP 3 · TEST

Small pieces travel further: an investigation

about 19 min · Criteria B and C
▶ Gel electrophoresis, step by step (DNALC)Watch for: which pieces travel furthest, and why?
  • The gel is like a jelly full of tiny holes. It works like a SIEVE.
  • SMALL pieces of DNA slip through the holes easily, so they travel FAR.
  • BIG pieces get stuck, so they stay near the TOP.
  • Rule: the LOWER the band, the SMALLER the piece.
  • Size is measured in BASE PAIRS (bp). 200 bp is smaller than 2000 bp.
  • In an investigation: the INDEPENDENT variable is what you change; the DEPENDENT variable is what you measure; CONTROL variables are kept the same.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Matching a sample, and finding the father

about 16 min · Criterion A
▶ Reading DNA profilesWatch for: how do you check if two profiles match?
  • Use a ruler. Check ONE height at a time.
  • A MATCH means EVERY band is at the same height in both lanes: all of them, not most of them.
  • If even ONE band is in a different place, or missing, the DNA is from a different person.
  • A profile that is "almost the same" is NOT a match. A close relative can have a very similar profile.

Who is the father?

▶ How to read DNA fingerprints and paternity testsWatch for: how can you tell which bands came from the father?
  • A child gets HALF of its DNA from its mother and HALF from its father.
  • So every band in the child must also be in the mother OR the father.
  • Step 1: find the child's bands that are also in the MOTHER. Those came from her.
  • Step 2: the child's OTHER bands must have come from the FATHER.
  • Step 3: the father is the man who has ALL of those other bands.
  • Do not just count how many bands a man shares with the child.

STEP 5 · CHALLENGE

DNA profiling and people

about 8 min · Criterion D
▶ How a family tree helped catch the Golden State KillerWatch for: how did police use DNA to find him? (Your teacher may play only part of this video.)
  • Police keep DNA profiles in a DATABASE. DNA from a crime scene can be checked against it.
  • BENEFITS: criminals are caught faster; innocent people who were wrongly sent to prison can be set free; victims of disasters can be identified.
  • CONCERNS: PRIVACY: your DNA also tells people about your family. Who can see it? Samples can be mixed up or CONTAMINATED.
  • A DNA match is EVIDENCE, not PROOF. DNA can get onto something for an innocent reason.
A real case
In 2018, police in the USA solved serious crimes from the 1970s and 1980s. They put DNA from a crime scene into a family-history website. They found the criminal's relatives first, and then found him.
