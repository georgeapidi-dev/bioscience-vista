STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Collect the data

10 – 30 min · Question 2
  • Follow your own procedure step by step. If you change it, write down what you changed: that is honest science.
  • Do every temperature THREE times: one reading could be a fluke.
  • Write every number down the moment you read it. Never keep a number in your head.
  • Write down what you actually measured, even if it looks wrong. Scientists do not tidy away results they do not like.
  • Safety: water no hotter than 60 °C, poured by the teacher; goggles on; beakers in the middle of the bench; nothing is tasted.

STEP 3 · TEST

Build the results table

30 – 40 min · Question 3

🎬 Videos for this lesson

▶ Means and anomaliesHow to work out a mean, and what to do with a result that does not fit
  • The thing you changed goes in the FIRST column, smallest at the top.
  • Units go in the heading, in brackets, for example time (s). Never write "s" after every number.
  • Add a last column for the MEAN: add the three trials and divide by 3.
  • An anomaly is one result nowhere near the other two. Circle it, leave it in the table, and leave it OUT of the mean.
  • Rule every line, and put a number in every box.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Draw the graph

40 – 55 min · Question 4
▶ Graphing in five minutesWhat every graph needs
▶ How to draw a line graph, step by stepChoosing a scale and plotting the points
  • If the thing you changed is in GROUPS with names (types of sugar, colours, brands), draw a BAR CHART with gaps between the bars.
  • If it is NUMBERS on a scale (temperature, height, time, mass), draw a LINE GRAPH.
  • Plot the MEAN, not all three trials. The thing you changed goes along the bottom; the thing you measured goes up the side.
  • Choose a scale in equal steps that fills most of the paper.

Notes

Describe a pattern with this shape: "As the ____ (what you changed) increased, the ____ (what you measured) ____ (went up / went down / stayed the same)." Then add two numbers from your table. A conclusion has three parts: ANSWER the research question; give EVIDENCE (two numbers); say whether the results SUPPORT your hypothesis. If they do not, say so honestly: you lose no marks for a hypothesis that turned out to be wrong.
