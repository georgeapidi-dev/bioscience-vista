STEP 2 · EXPLORE

The stages of mitosis

15 – 30 min · Explain

📺 Watch and learn

Watch before you answer, or afterwards to check your understanding. Plays here, inside the lesson.

Cell Division: Mitosis and Meiosis (GCSE Biology)plays hereMitosis vs Meiosis Explainedplays here
  • MITOSIS is cell division that produces TWO genetically IDENTICAL daughter cells, each with the same number of chromosomes as the parent cell (diploid, 46 in humans).
  • INTERPHASE (before mitosis): the cell grows, makes new organelles and COPIES its DNA, so each chromosome becomes two identical SISTER CHROMATIDS joined at a centromere.
  • PROPHASE: chromosomes condense and become visible; the nuclear membrane breaks down; spindle fibres form.
  • METAPHASE: chromosomes line up across the MIDDLE of the cell, attached to the spindle.
  • ANAPHASE: the spindle fibres pull the sister chromatids APART to opposite poles.
  • TELOPHASE: new nuclear membranes form round each set of chromosomes. CYTOKINESIS: the cytoplasm divides into two cells.
  • Memory aid: I P M A T (+ C). Metaphase = Middle; Anaphase = Apart; Telophase = Two nuclei.

Why mitosis matters, and its limits

  • IMPORTANCE: GROWTH (one zygote becomes trillions of cells), REPAIR and REPLACEMENT of damaged or worn-out cells, and ASEXUAL REPRODUCTION (bacteria, potatoes, strawberry runners).
  • LIMITATION 1: if DNA is copied wrongly, the error (a MUTATION) is passed to every daughter cell. Mutations in genes that control division can make cells divide uncontrollably: a TUMOUR, which may become CANCER.
  • LIMITATION 2: mitosis makes NO GENETIC VARIATION. Every daughter cell is a copy, so a population produced by mitosis alone cannot adapt to a new disease or a changing environment.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Connect: meiosis

45 – 55 min · Connect
▶ MeiosisCaptions available. Work on your own or with a partner.
  • MEIOSIS is cell division that makes GAMETES (sperm and eggs; pollen and ovules in plants). It happens only in the testes and ovaries (anthers and ovaries in plants).
  • It has TWO divisions, producing FOUR daughter cells, each HAPLOID: half the chromosome number (23 in humans).
  • The daughter cells are all GENETICALLY DIFFERENT. In meiosis I, matching chromosomes pair up and swap sections (CROSSING OVER), and the pairs line up and separate in a random way (independent assortment).
  • Halving the number matters: at fertilisation two gametes fuse, restoring the diploid number (23 + 23 = 46). Without meiosis the number would double every generation.
