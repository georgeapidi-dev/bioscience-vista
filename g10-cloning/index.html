STEP 1 · QUESTION

What is a clone?

about 10 min · Part 1
Answer this BEFORE you read anything else
Do human clones already exist? Write YES or NO with one sentence saying why. Do not look ahead and do not change it afterwards: you will come back to it in Step 2.

In your book, then here

Hook. Do human clones already exist? YES or NO, and one sentence why. not marked
  • A CLONE is a genetically identical individual: exactly the same DNA, and so the same genes, as the individual it was made from.
  • Clones are produced by MITOSIS, which copies the DNA exactly and makes identical daughter cells.
  • Sexual reproduction cannot produce clones: MEIOSIS shuffles the genes into gametes, and FERTILISATION combines DNA from two different parents.
  • The rule: one parent and mitosis gives clones; two parents, meiosis and fertilisation gives variation.
  • NATURAL cloning happens by itself (asexual reproduction in plants, identical twins, binary fission in bacteria). ARTIFICIAL cloning is done deliberately: copying a piece of DNA, a group of cells, or a whole organism.

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Natural cloning: plants, and identical twins

about 14 min · Part 2
  • IN PLANTS: part of the parent grows, by mitosis, into a complete new plant, so the new plant is a clone.
  • POTATO: underground stem TUBERS each grow into a new plant. GINGER and turmeric use RHIZOMES.
  • STRAWBERRY: a sideways stem called a RUNNER roots where it touches down.
  • ONION and garlic use BULBS. BANANA plants produce SUCKERS at the base, which is how almost all bananas are grown.
  • Advantages: fast, no pollinator or partner needed, and every offspring inherits what made the parent successful. Disadvantage: no variation.
  • IN ANIMALS: IDENTICAL TWINS are natural clones. One egg + one sperm make one zygote, which SPLITS into two very early. Same DNA, always the same sex.
  • NON-IDENTICAL TWINS are not clones: two eggs, two different sperm, two zygotes. They share about half their genes, like any siblings, and may be different sexes.

STEP 3 · TEST

Artificial cloning: DNA, cells and whole organisms

about 14 min · Part 3

🎬 Videos for this lesson

▶ Cloning plants: micropropagation (tissue culture)Five minutes. Watch before Question 4.
  • COPYING A DNA FRAGMENT: PCR (the polymerase chain reaction) makes millions of copies of one piece of DNA in a few hours, turning a trace of crime-scene DNA into enough for a profile. Genes can also be put into a plasmid in bacteria, which multiply: this is how human insulin is made.
  • COPYING CELLS: in TISSUE CULTURE a small piece of tissue (an EXPLANT) is placed on STERILE agar with nutrients and plant hormones. The cells divide by mitosis into many tiny plantlets, all clones. Everything must be sterile, or bacteria and fungi would outgrow the plant cells.
  • COPYING A WHOLE ORGANISM: in plants, CUTTINGS; in animals, somatic cell nuclear transfer (Step 4).
  • In KENYA: banana suckers carry the parent's diseases, and yields fall year after year. Tissue-culture seedlings come from healthy parents, start disease-free, grow faster and give a bigger harvest; thousands can be made from one good parent.

Three sheep: A gave the body cell, B gave the egg, C carried the pregnancy.
  • In 1996, at the Roslin Institute in Edinburgh, Dolly became the first mammal cloned from an adult body cell, by SOMATIC CELL NUCLEAR TRANSFER (somatic = a body cell, not a gamete).
  • Sheep A (WHITE face) gave the body cell. Sheep B (BLACK face) gave the egg cell. Sheep C (black face) was the SURROGATE mother.
  • The nucleus was taken from A's body cell. The nucleus was removed from B's egg, leaving an empty egg. A's nucleus was put into the empty egg.
  • A small electric shock made the cell divide by mitosis, forming an embryo, which was implanted into the uterus of sheep C. A lamb was born.
  • Dolly had a WHITE face: her characteristics came from sheep A, the nucleus donor. The nucleus carries the DNA, so the nucleus decides.
  • 277 fused cells were made and only one produced a live lamb. Cloned animals often have health problems.

STEP 5 · CHALLENGE

Benefits, risks and ethics

about 16 min · Part 5 · Criterion D practice
  • AGRICULTURE: thousands of identical, high-yielding, disease-free plants from one good parent; a prize animal's qualities reproduced exactly.
  • MEDICINE: PCR gives enough DNA to diagnose disease and make DNA profiles; cloned bacteria make human insulin; therapeutic cloning may grow replacement tissue the body will not reject.
  • CONSERVATION: cloning is being tried to increase numbers of endangered species.
  • RISK, NO VARIATION: every clone has the same weaknesses. In Ireland in 1845 the potato crop was almost one clone; blight arrived and about a million people died. Today's export banana is a single clone under threat from a soil fungus.
  • No variation also means nothing for natural selection or selective breeding to work on.
  • ANIMAL WELFARE: very low success rates, many embryos and surrogates per live birth, and cloned animals often have health problems.
  • ETHICS: cloning humans is banned in almost every country. Who decides, and who can afford these technologies, are questions science alone cannot answer.
