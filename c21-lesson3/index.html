STEP 1 · QUESTION

GPCRs

0 – 16 min · C2.1.9
  • G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) are transmembrane receptors (crossing the membrane seven times) linked to a G protein on the inside.
  • When a ligand binds, the receptor changes shape and activates the G protein, which swaps GDP for GTP; part of the G protein then moves along the membrane and activates an enzyme or channel.
  • There are hundreds of GPCRs in humans (for smell, taste, light, many hormones and neurotransmitters); about a third of drugs act on them.

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Adrenaline and cAMP

16 – 34 min · C2.1.10
  • Adrenaline (epinephrine) binds to a GPCR on liver and muscle cells.
  • The activated G protein activates adenylyl cyclase, which converts ATP into cyclic AMP (cAMP), the second messenger.
  • cAMP activates protein kinase A, which starts a phosphorylation cascade that activates enzymes breaking down glycogen to glucose. The cascade amplifies the signal greatly.
  • Nature of science: both “adrenaline” (Latin, used in Europe) and “epinephrine” (Greek, used in the USA) are accepted; agreed naming conventions are an example of international cooperation.

STEP 3 · TEST

Insulin and tyrosine kinase

34 – 52 min · C2.1.11
  • The insulin receptor is a transmembrane receptor with tyrosine kinase activity.
  • When insulin (a protein hormone) binds outside, the receptor’s inside parts phosphorylate tyrosine residues (on itself and other proteins).
  • This sets off a sequence of reactions ending with vesicles carrying GLUT4 (glucose transporter) moving to and fusing with the plasma membrane.
  • More glucose transporters → more glucose uptake by facilitated diffusion into muscle and fat cells, lowering blood glucose.
