STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Connecting the organs to meiosis: the chromosome number problem

38 – 60 min · Case study
  • Human body cells are DIPLOID: 46 chromosomes, in 23 pairs.
  • The DNA is copied ONCE, before meiosis I begins.
  • The cell then divides TWICE: meiosis I and meiosis II.
  • The result is FOUR HAPLOID cells, each with 23 chromosomes.
  • At fertilisation 23 + 23 = 46, so the diploid number is restored.
  • Meiosis also produces genetic variation, so no two gametes are identical.

Amina's investigation
Amina, a Grade 10 student, writes in her notebook: "Human body cells contain 46 chromosomes. If a sperm cell and an egg cell were also diploid and each contained 46, the fertilised cell would contain 92 chromosomes. The generation after that would have 184. But humans have had 46 chromosomes for as long as we have been able to measure. Why doesn't the number keep doubling?"
Her reasoning about the numbers is completely correct. What she is missing is one biological process.
