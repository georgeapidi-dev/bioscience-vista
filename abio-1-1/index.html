STEP 1 · QUESTION

Slides and drawings

0 – 8 min · 1.1.1 – 1.1.2
  • Temporary slide: a thin layer of tissue (e.g. onion epidermis) on a slide, a drop of stain (iodine for starch and nuclei; methylene blue for animal cells), and a coverslip lowered at an angle to avoid air bubbles.
  • Biological drawings: sharp pencil, clear continuous lines, no shading, correct proportions, label lines ruled and not crossing.
  • Draw what you see, not what a textbook shows.

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Magnification and actual size

8 – 20 min · 1.1.3 – 1.1.4
  • Magnification = image size ÷ actual size (I = M × A).
  • Units: 1 mm = 1 000 µm; 1 µm = 1 000 nm. Convert to the same unit first.
  • An eyepiece graticule is a scale in the eyepiece with no units; calibrate it against a stage micrometer (a slide with a scale of known divisions, often 0.01 mm) for each objective lens.
✏️ Worked example: Calibrating a graticule
  1. 10 stage micrometer divisions (each 10 µm) line up with 40 eyepiece units.
  2. 100 µm = 40 eyepiece units.
  3. 1 eyepiece unit = 100 ÷ 40 = 2.5 µm.
  4. A cell 18 eyepiece units long is 18 × 2.5 = 45 µm.

STEP 3 · TEST

Magnification versus resolution

20 – 32 min · 1.1.5
  • Magnification: how many times larger the image is than the object.
  • Resolution: the ability to distinguish two points that are close together as separate.
  • Resolution is limited by wavelength: light ≈ 200 nm; electron microscopes ≈ 0.5 nm (TEM).
  • Increasing magnification beyond the limit of resolution only gives a bigger blur.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Light and electron microscopes

32 – 46 min · 1.1.5
  • Light microscope: living or dead specimens, colour, cheap, resolution ≈ 200 nm, maximum useful magnification ≈ ×1 500.
  • TEM: thin sections, electrons pass through; 2D images of internal ultrastructure; resolution ≈ 0.5 nm.
  • SEM: electrons scattered from the surface; 3D surface images; resolution ≈ 3–10 nm.
  • Electron microscopes need a vacuum, so specimens are dead; images are black and white (colour is added).
