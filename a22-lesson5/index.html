Endosymbiosis and its evidence

5 – 20 min · A2.2.12

📺 Watch and learn

Watch before you answer, or afterwards to check your understanding. Plays here, inside the lesson.

Endosymbiotic theoryAmoeba Sisters · 5:24 · plays hereHow cells become specialised (stem cells)Amoeba Sisters · 6:51 · plays here
  • ENDOSYMBIOTIC THEORY: mitochondria evolved from aerobic bacteria (related to proteobacteria) engulfed by a larger host cell by endocytosis; chloroplasts evolved later from engulfed photosynthetic cyanobacteria, in one lineage.
  • The engulfed cell was not digested; both partners benefited (mutualism). Over time many of its genes moved to the host nucleus.
  • All eukaryotes have a common unicellular ancestor that already had a nucleus and mitochondria.
  • EVIDENCE: double membrane (inner from the bacterium, outer from the vesicle); their own circular, naked DNA; 70S ribosomes like bacteria; they divide by binary fission and are not made by the cell from scratch; their DNA and ribosomal RNA sequences are most similar to bacteria; antibiotics that affect bacteria affect them.

STEP 3 · TEST

Differentiation and gene expression

20 – 35 min · A2.2.13
  • Every cell in a multicellular organism contains the same genome (with rare exceptions such as red blood cells).
  • DIFFERENTIATION: cells develop along different pathways and become specialised for a function.
  • It happens because different cells EXPRESS (switch on) different genes. Expressed genes are transcribed and translated into the proteins that give the cell its structure and function.
  • Which genes are expressed is decided by signals: for example the cell's position in the embryo and chemical signals from neighbouring cells.
  • Stem cells are unspecialised cells that can divide and differentiate.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Multicellularity

35 – 45 min · A2.2.14
  • Multicellularity evolved INDEPENDENTLY many times: in animals, land plants, fungi, red algae, brown algae (kelps) and some others. These lineages did not share a multicellular ancestor.
  • ADVANTAGES: larger body size; longer life, because cells can be replaced; and SPECIALISATION of cells for different functions (division of labour), making the organism more efficient.
  • POSSIBLE STAGES: single cells → cells stay together after division (colony, like Gonium) → some cells specialise for reproduction while others do not (Volvox) → many specialised cell types, with cells that depend on each other and cannot survive alone.
