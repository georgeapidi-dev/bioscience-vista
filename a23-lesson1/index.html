  • Viruses are non-cellular (acellular) infectious particles.
  • They lack the features used to classify organisms, so they sit outside the three domains (Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya).
  • Outside a cell a virus is inert: no metabolism, no respiration, no growth.
  • Inside a host cell it can direct the cell to make thousands of copies.
  • Small: about 20–300 nm, far smaller than prokaryotic cells; seen only with an electron microscope.
  • Fixed size: made of few molecules, viruses do not grow.
  • Nucleic acid core: DNA or RNA (never both in the particle), single- or double-stranded, linear or circular.
  • Capsid: a protein coat; attachment proteins on the outside bind specific host cells.
  • No cytoplasm and few or no enzymes: no metabolism of their own.
  • Some viruses have an outer lipid envelope around the capsid.
  • The envelope comes from the membrane of the host cell the virus left (plasma membrane or another internal membrane).
  • Envelope glycoproteins help the virus recognise and enter host cells.
  • Enveloped: HIV, influenza, coronaviruses. Non-enveloped: adenovirus, bacteriophages.
  • Viruses reproduce only inside living host cells: they are obligate intracellular parasites.
  • They use the host's ribosomes, tRNA, amino acids, nucleotides and enzymes to make viral proteins and copy their genome.
  • The energy (ATP) comes from the host cell's respiration; viruses do not respire.
  • This works because viruses use the same genetic code as their hosts, so host ribosomes translate viral mRNA correctly.

By the end of this lesson I will be able to

  • Describe the structural features shared by all viruses: small, fixed size; DNA or RNA; a protein capsid; no cytoplasm; few or no enzymes.
  • Explain why viruses cannot reproduce on their own.
  • Explain obligate parasitism and the dependence of viruses on host cells.
  • Explain why a shared genetic code lets viruses use host machinery.

  • SMALL, FIXED SIZE: about 20–300 nm, smaller than a bacterium; viruses do not grow.
  • NUCLEIC ACID as genetic material: DNA or RNA, never both in the virion.
  • A CAPSID made of protein subunits (capsomeres) that protects the nucleic acid and helps it enter a host cell.
  • NO CYTOPLASM: no ribosomes, no metabolism of their own.
  • FEW OR NO ENZYMES: some carry one or two (for example reverse transcriptase in HIV), but none can make ATP or proteins.
  • Some viruses also have an ENVELOPE taken from the host cell membrane; many do not.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Obligate parasites

30 – 45 min · Teacher-guided explanation
  • An OBLIGATE PARASITE can only reproduce inside a host cell.
  • A virus depends on the host for ENERGY (ATP), NUTRITION (nucleotides and amino acids) and PROTEIN SYNTHESIS (ribosomes, tRNA, enzymes).
  • The GENETIC CODE is shared: the same codons code for the same amino acids in viruses and living organisms, so host ribosomes can read viral genes and make viral proteins.
  • This is the answer to the guiding question: viruses need very few genes because the host cell supplies almost everything else.
