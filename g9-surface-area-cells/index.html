STEP 3 · EXPLAIN

Why size matters to a cell

20 min · Explain
  • SURFACE AREA is the total area of the outside of the cell. It is the "doorway": the size of the membrane through which substances enter and leave.
  • VOLUME is the amount of space inside the cell. It tells you how much living material must be supplied with oxygen and food, and how much waste is produced.
  • The SA : V ratio compares the two. A high ratio means a large doorway for a small inside (good). A low ratio means a small doorway for a large inside (a problem).

Everything must cross the cell membrane

  • Oxygen, water and nutrients move INTO the cell across the membrane, mostly by DIFFUSION.
  • Carbon dioxide and other waste products move OUT across the same membrane.
  • Diffusion is slow over long distances. In a large cell, substances take much longer to reach the centre.
The maths behind the problem: when the side doubles (1 cm → 2 cm)
Surface area 6 → 24 cm²: 4 times bigger (side × side). Volume 1 → 8 cm³: 8 times bigger (side × side × side). So the volume grows FASTER, and SA : V falls from 6 : 1 to 3 : 1.
Key idea. As a cell gets larger, its volume increases faster than its surface area, so the SA : V ratio decreases. A large cell cannot take in enough oxygen and nutrients, or remove waste quickly enough, for everything inside it. This is why cells cannot keep growing; instead, they divide
