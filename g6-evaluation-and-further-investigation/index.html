1 (c). What did your results show? One sentence with one number in it. [1]
Support hintInclude a number from your table.
This step: 1 marks
Notes

An investigation is VALID when you really measured what you set out to measure, and the only thing that changed was your independent variable. So a valid investigation must be a fair test. Some controlled variables are easy to keep the same (the volume of water, the mass of sugar); others slip: water cools down, the person timing changes. A controlled variable that slipped is not cheating: write it down honestly and explain it.

This step: 1 marks
Notes

Reliable means repeatable: if you did the same test again, you would get nearly the same number. Look at your three trials for ONE temperature. Close together (within a few seconds) means reliable. Far apart means something was different each time. One anomaly does not ruin an investigation: it is evidence about the method.

Notes

An improvement must fix a problem you have already named: write the problem, then the improvement, then why it helps. It must be something you could actually do next week. "Be more careful" and "use better equipment" are not improvements: say the actual change. Useful ideas: repeat more times; use a water bath; one person times every trial; agree a rule for when the sugar has gone; use a white tile; use a balance that reads to 0.1 g.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Errors, limitations and improvements

40 – 60 min · Questions 4 and 5
▶ Accuracy and precision in scienceClose to the true answer, or consistent? Two words scientists never mix up
ErrorLimitation
What it isA mistake in the measuring that could have been avoided and put right next timeSomething the method could not do, however carefully you worked
ExampleThe stopwatch was started a moment lateThe water cools while the sugar dissolves, and a beaker cannot stop that
The testCould a careful person have avoided it? YESCould a careful person have avoided it? NO

This step: 10 marks
Notes

Every investigation ends with a new question. Anything you kept the same could be changed on purpose next time. Ideas: the size of the sugar grains; the amount of water; the type of sugar; stirring or not; salt instead of sugar. Your new question must be testable with a measurement you can make. Use: "How does changing ____ affect ____?"
