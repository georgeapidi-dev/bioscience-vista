STEP 2 · EXPLORE

The pollen tube and fertilisation

5 – 22 min · Criterion A

🎬 Videos for this lesson

▶ Fertilisation, seed formation and dispersal (Cognito)The whole of today in seven minutes. Watch before Questions 2 and 3.
Figure 1 · From a pollen grain on the stigma to a seed and a fruit
  • A pollen grain lands on the STIGMA. If it is the right species, the sticky stigma holds it and the grain begins to grow.
  • The grain grows a POLLEN TUBE down through the STYLE towards an ovule. The male gamete nucleus travels down inside the tube.
  • The tube enters the ovule through a tiny hole, the MICROPYLE. The male gamete nucleus FUSES with the female gamete nucleus: FERTILISATION. The cell formed is the ZYGOTE.
  • Pollination is TRANSFER; fertilisation is FUSION. Pollination always comes first, and does not guarantee fertilisation will follow.
  • Plant male gametes cannot swim: no tail, and no water to swim through. The pollen tube carries the gamete to the egg instead.
  • Each fertilised ovule becomes one seed: a pea pod holds several seeds because its ovary held several ovules, each reached by its own pollen tube.

STEP 3 · TEST

Seed and fruit formation

22 – 36 min · Before and after
  • After fertilisation four things happen at once, each with a "before" and an "after" name. Learn them as PAIRS.
  • The ZYGOTE divides by mitosis into the EMBRYO: the tiny plant, with a young root and a young shoot.
  • The OVULE becomes the SEED: a tough SEED COAT (testa) forms round the embryo and a FOOD STORE, usually starch.
  • The OVARY becomes the FRUIT: its wall thickens, and how it thickens decides how the seed will travel.
  • Petals, stamens, sepals and stigma wither away. A fruit is the ripened ovary, so a tomato, a cucumber and a pea pod are all fruits in biology.
  • A seed is not a plant that has stopped growing; it is one that has not started: dry, DORMANT and able to wait until conditions are right.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Why seeds must be dispersed

36 – 45 min · Criterion A
  • DISPERSAL is the scattering of seeds away from the parent plant.
  • A seedling under its parent competes with it for LIGHT, WATER and MINERAL IONS, and loses, because the parent is bigger. Seeds that stay also compete with each other.
  • Dispersal lets a species colonise new ground, and spreads a population out so one disease, fire or grazing animal cannot destroy all of it.
Space is not a resource. "They will not have space" scores nothing on its own: name what they compete FOR

STEP 5 · CHALLENGE

How seeds are dispersed, germination and the complete cycle

45 – 68 min · Apply
  • WIND: very light seeds with wings or parachutes (sycamore, dandelion).
  • ANIMAL, HOOKS: hooks catch on fur and are rubbed off elsewhere (burdock, cleavers).
  • ANIMAL, EATEN: sweet, bright, fleshy fruit; the hard seeds pass out undamaged in the droppings, with fertiliser.
  • WATER: light, airtight, waterproof fruit that floats (coconut).
  • EXPLOSIVE: the dry pod dries unevenly, splits suddenly and flicks the seeds out (peas, beans, gorse).
  • Read the method off the fruit: wings mean wind, hooks mean fur, sweet and fleshy means eaten.

Germination and the complete cycle

▶ Plant reproduction: germination (Cognito)What a seed needs before it will grow.
  • GERMINATION is the start of growth of the seed into a seedling. It needs WATER, OXYGEN and a SUITABLE TEMPERATURE. Light is NOT needed: most seeds germinate underground.
  • Water rehydrates the tissues and lets enzymes work; oxygen is needed for aerobic respiration; the temperature must suit the enzymes.
  • The seed lives on its food store until the first leaves open, because the young plant cannot photosynthesise yet.
  • Chromosome numbers: adult plant diploid, gametes haploid, zygote diploid, and every cell built from the zygote by mitosis diploid. Exactly as in humans.
