Notes

Mass is the amount of matter in an object, measured with an electronic balance in grams (g) or kilograms (kg). It is the same everywhere in the universe. Weight is a force: the pull of gravity on an object, measured with a newton meter (spring balance) in newtons (N). Weight = mass × gravitational field strength (W = m × g). On Earth g = 10 N/kg, so a 2 kg bag of sugar weighs 2 × 10 = 20 N. On the Moon g is about 1.6 N/kg, so it weighs 3.2 N, but its mass is still 2 kg.

Notes

To find a mean, add the three trials and divide by 3, then round to one decimal place: 1.0 + 1.1 + 0.9 = 3.0, and 3.0 ÷ 3 = 1.0 N. On the graph, mass (the independent variable) goes across and mean weight (the dependent variable) goes up. Label both axes with quantity and unit, use more than half the paper, plot small neat crosses, draw one ruled line of best fit, and title it "Effect of Mass on Weight".
