1.1 What is digital society? · Lesson 4: Milestones II: the internet, online social networks, mobile and cloud computing

IB Diploma Digital Society · 1.1 What is digital society? · Lesson 4 · 1.1C · SL and HL · 90 minutes · Prepared by VistaEd
Guiding question: How did connecting computers, people and phones create today's digital society?
Virtual labsGraph and drawing tools
🎯 Start here

In 1969 a few university computers in the United States were linked. By 2007 a phone could browse the web; by 2023 several billion people used social networks and stored their photos in data centres they would never see. Connection, not just computation, made digital society.

STEP 1 · QUESTION

The internet and the web

0 – 10 min · 1.1C
  • The internet: a global network of networks; it began with ARPANET in 1969.
  • The World Wide Web: a system of linked pages on the internet, proposed by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN (Switzerland) in 1989.
  • The internet is the roads; the web is one service that travels on them (e-mail and video calls are others).

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Social networks, phones and the cloud

10 – 25 min · 1.1C
  • Online social networks (2000s onwards): people create and share content, not just read it.
  • Mobile computing: smartphones (mainstream from about 2007) and mobile data put the internet in billions of pockets; in many countries people went online by phone before ever using a PC.
  • Cloud computing: storage and software run in remote data centres and are reached over the internet.
✏️ Worked example: One day, four milestones
  1. 07:00: alarm on a smartphone (mobile computing).
  2. 08:00: class notes saved online (cloud computing).
  3. 13:00: lunch photo posted (online social network).
  4. 20:00: research on linked web pages (internet and web).

STEP 3 · TEST

Changing communication

25 – 40 min · 1.1C
  • Communication became instant, global and cheap: messaging apps replaced many letters and phone calls.
  • People became creators as well as consumers (user-generated content).
  • Mobile money and mobile learning reached people without banks or schools nearby.
