Egg and sperm cells

5 – 20 min · Structure fits function

🎬 Videos for this lesson

▶ Sperm cell adaptationsThree minutes on how the sperm is built for the journey.
  • Both gametes are HAPLOID: 23 chromosomes each, half the number in a body cell.
  • The sperm has to TRAVEL; the egg has to SUPPLY. Every difference follows from those two words.
  • The sperm is stripped down to a nucleus, an energy supply and a tail. The egg is large, packed with food reserves and protected by a coat.
  • Hundreds of millions of sperm are released at once; usually ONE egg is released each month.

STEP 3 · TEST

The human life cycle

20 – 32 min · Balance
  • A human body cell is DIPLOID: 46 chromosomes, in 23 pairs, one of each pair from each parent.
  • MEIOSIS in the testes and ovaries halves that number: HAPLOID gametes with 23.
  • FERTILISATION joins two haploid nuclei and restores the diploid number: 23 + 23 = 46, the ZYGOTE.
  • MITOSIS then divides the zygote again and again, without changing the number, to build an embryo, fetus and adult.
  • Meiosis halves, fertilisation restores. Without meiosis the number would double every generation.
  • Sexual reproduction also produces VARIATION: each gamete has a different combination of chromosomes, and fertilisation is random.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Copulation and fertilisation

32 – 48 min · Describe in order
▶ Sexual reproduction in humans (IGCSE Biology 16.4)Copulation, fertilisation and early development in the correct scientific language.
  • COPULATION (sexual intercourse) transfers semen containing sperm from the male into the female reproductive tract: INTERNAL fertilisation, which protects the gametes and needs far fewer of them.
  • Sperm swim from the vagina through the CERVIX, across the UTERUS and into the OVIDUCTS. Of hundreds of millions, only a few hundred reach the egg.
  • FERTILISATION is the fusion of a haploid sperm nucleus with a haploid egg nucleus, forming a diploid zygote. In humans it happens in the OVIDUCT.
  • The ACROSOME releases enzymes that digest a path through the jelly coat. The first sperm to reach the membrane fuses with it, and only its NUCLEUS enters.
  • The jelly coat changes and hardens immediately, so no second sperm can enter: two sperm would give 69 chromosomes and the embryo would not survive.
  • The zygote divides by mitosis as it travels down the oviduct and, about seven days later, IMPLANTS in the thickened uterus lining.

STEP 5 · CHALLENGE

Gestation, the placenta and lactation

48 – 70 min · Apply
  • GESTATION is the development inside the uterus, from implantation to birth: about 38 weeks from fertilisation (40 from the last period), roughly nine months.
  • The embryo becomes a FETUS at about eight weeks, once the main organs have formed.
  • The PLACENTA grows from both the embryo and the uterus lining. Fetal VILLI project into the mother's blood spaces: a very large surface area and a very short diffusion distance.
  • The fetus receives oxygen, glucose, amino acids, vitamins, minerals and antibodies; carbon dioxide and urea pass the other way.
  • The two blood supplies come very close but NEVER MIX: pressures stay separate, the mother's immune system does not attack the fetus, and fetal blood is not lost.
  • The UMBILICAL CORD carries blood between fetus and placenta. AMNIOTIC FLUID cushions the fetus, supports it and keeps its temperature steady.
  • Alcohol, nicotine, many drugs and some viruses DO cross the placenta, which is why they affect the fetus.

Lactation

  • LACTATION is the production and release of milk by the MAMMARY GLANDS after birth: one of the features that defines a mammal.
  • PROLACTIN stimulates milk production; OXYTOCIN releases the milk when the baby suckles. Suckling triggers more of both, so supply matches demand.
  • The first fluid is COLOSTRUM: thick, yellowish, very rich in ANTIBODIES and protein, giving the newborn PASSIVE IMMUNITY.
  • Breast milk supplies all the nutrients a baby needs for about six months, at the right temperature and free of contamination, and changes as the baby grows.
