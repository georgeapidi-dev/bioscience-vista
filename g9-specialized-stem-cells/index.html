STEP 3 · EXPLAIN

Specialization, differentiation and stem cells

20 min · Explain

What is a specialized cell?

  • A SPECIALIZED CELL is a cell whose structure is adapted to carry out one particular job.
  • In a multicellular organism the jobs are shared out: no single cell has to do everything, so each type of cell can be very good at one thing.
  • The key idea: STRUCTURE IS RELATED TO FUNCTION. If you know what a cell looks like, you can often work out what it does.
Worked example: root hair cell
Structure: a long, thin extension that pushes out into the soil. → Adaptation: this greatly increases the surface area of the cell. → Function: more surface means more water and mineral ions can be absorbed from the soil.

Differentiation and stem cells

  • All the cells in your body started as UNSPECIALIZED cells.
  • DIFFERENTIATION is the process by which an unspecialized cell develops the structures it needs to do one particular job. Once a cell has differentiated, it usually keeps that job for life.
  • A STEM CELL is an unspecialized cell. It can divide by MITOSIS to produce new cells.
  • Some of those new cells stay as stem cells: this is SELF-RENEWAL, and it keeps the supply going. Others differentiate into specialized cells.
  • Not all stem cells can become every kind of cell. Stem cells in BONE MARROW produce blood cells; stem cells in the tips of plant roots and shoots produce plant cells such as root hair cells.
Careful: a common mistake. It is NOT true that "stem cells are the only cells that can divide by mitosis". Many specialized cells divide too: skin cells do it constantly. Some highly specialized cells divide little or not at all: a mature red blood cell has no nucleus, so it cannot divide and must be replaced by new cells made in the bone marrow

Real-life connection
Patients with some blood cancers can have a bone marrow transplant: healthy stem cells are given so they can make working blood cells again. Gardeners use the same biology when they take a cutting: stem cells at a shoot tip produce the new cells a whole plant needs.
