STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Induced fit and collisions

15 min
Memory hook: Lock-and-key = key in lock (rigid) · Induced fit = hand in a glove (both adjust)
Worked example Distinguish between the lock-and-key and induced-fit models of enzyme action. [2]
Model answer
  • Lock-and-key: active site has a fixed/rigid shape exactly complementary to the substrate ✓
  • Induced fit: enzyme and substrate both change shape on binding / active site moulds around the substrate, stressing its bonds ✓
Memory hook: Collide · Correct orientation · Complex forms
Worked example Explain why an increase in temperature from 20 °C to 30 °C increases the rate of an enzyme-catalysed reaction. [3]
Model answer
  • Molecules gain kinetic energy / move faster ✓
  • so substrate collides with the active site more frequently ✓
  • more enzyme–substrate complexes form per unit time / more collisions have enough energy ✓

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Specificity, denaturation and activation energy

20 min
Memory hook: Shape → Site → Specificity. Break the shape, lose the site.
Worked example Explain how a high temperature affects enzyme activity. [3]
Model answer
  • Bonds holding the tertiary structure (hydrogen/ionic bonds) break due to increased vibration ✓
  • the active site changes shape / enzyme is denatured ✓
  • substrate can no longer bind / fewer enzyme–substrate complexes so rate falls ✓
Memory hook: Enzymes lower the hill, not the drop.
Worked example On an energy diagram, the substrate is at 60 kJ, the peak without enzyme is at 140 kJ, the peak with enzyme is at 90 kJ and the products are at 20 kJ. Calculate (a) activation energy with enzyme, (b) net energy released. [2]
Model answer
  • (a) 90 − 60 = 30 kJ ✓
  • (b) 60 − 20 = 40 kJ released (same with or without enzyme) ✓
