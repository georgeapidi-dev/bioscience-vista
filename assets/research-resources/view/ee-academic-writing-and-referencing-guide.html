Structure of an extended essay

The recognised structure is: title page, contents page, introduction, body of the essay, conclusion, and reference list or bibliography. Add the structural conventions of your subject (for a science essay, for example, method, results and discussion).

Examiners do not read beyond 4,000 words, so an essay over the limit is compromised across all criteria.

Writing analytically

  • Each paragraph makes a point that answers part of your research question, gives evidence, and explains what the evidence shows.

  • Link paragraphs so a clear, sustained line of argument runs from the research question through the findings to the conclusion (criterion C).

  • Discuss the significance of findings with evidence, and evaluate the strengths and limitations of your research (criterion D).

  • Use subject terminology accurately and consistently (criterion B).

Citing while writing

Cite every idea, quotation, image, data set or method that is not yours, at the point you use it, and list it in the references. Use one style consistently. Example (APA 7):

Type In the text In the reference list
Journal article (Smith & Lee, 2020) Smith, J., & Lee, K. (2020). Title of the article. Journal Name, 12(3), 45–60. https://doi.org/…
Book (Campbell et al., 2021) Campbell, N. A., et al. (2021). Title of the book (12th ed.). Publisher.
Website (World Health Organization, 2024) World Health Organization. (2024). Title of the page. https://… (accessed 18 November 2026)

AI and integrity

The guide states that using AI to write an essay that is then presented as your own is dishonest. AI-generated material may be one of your resources, but it must be checked against other sources, acknowledged and cited. Record any AI use as your school requires, and never use it to invent data or references.

Spell-checkers and reference generators are allowed, but you must check their suggestions.
