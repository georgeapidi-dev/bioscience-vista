Before you start

IA requirements: IB DP Biology guide, first assessment 2025 (for May/Nov 2027 and 2028): 20% of the grade; 24 marks (research design, data analysis, conclusion, evaluation; 6 each); maximum 3,000 words; same at SL and HL.

Headings are a guide, not compulsory: organise the report so it reads clearly. Keep within 3,000 words (tables, graphs, equations, the reference list and appendices are not usually counted, but check with your teacher).

Title page

A catchy title, then a subtitle: "How does (range of the independent variable, with units) affect (dependent variable, with units) in (organism, Latin name in italics)?"

Introduction

Research question

Described within a specific and appropriate context: name the IV with range and units, the DV and how it is measured, and the organism or system.

Say why you chose it. Show personal engagement and international-mindedness: how does it relate to your local and global community? Mention the preliminary trial that showed it was feasible.

Background information

A concise description of the system and the background theory of direct relevance. At least 3 sources, ideally previous studies in academic journals rather than websites.

Hypothesis

State a null hypothesis (no difference or no relationship) and an alternative hypothesis, with a biological explanation of your prediction.

Research design

Variables

Independent, dependent and controlled variables. Justify the scope, quantity and quality of measurements: range and interval of the IV (aim for at least 5 levels) and repeats (aim for at least 5).

Apparatus

A list or table, with the size and uncertainty of each measuring instrument (e.g. 10 cm³ syringe ± 0.1 cm³).

Method

Numbered steps in the past tense, detailed enough for someone else to reproduce the investigation: quantities, times, temperatures, repeats.

Risk assessment: safety, ethics and environment

Safety: a risk table (hazard, risk, likelihood × severity, control). Ethics: how living organisms and any plant material were ethically sourced and treated. Environment: impact and disposal of materials.

Data analysis

Raw data

Usually a table: every table and graph has a title; units and uncertainties in the column headings; values to the precision of the instrument. Include qualitative observations (descriptions or photographs).

Data processing

Means, standard deviations and uncertainties, with a sample calculation. SD is meaningful only with enough repeats (5 or more). Choose a statistical test from your design: correlation → Spearman's rank or Pearson; 2 means → t-test; more than 2 means → ANOVA (then a post-hoc test); counts against expected counts → chi-squared.

Impact of uncertainty

Instrument precision: ± the smallest division (analogue scales are often read to half a division). Show how uncertainty affects processed values and your confidence in the pattern.

Interpretation of processed data

Graphs with titles, labelled axes with units and error bars (say what they show, usually ± 1 SD). State clearly whether the null hypothesis is rejected or not. Tests support a hypothesis; they never prove it.

Conclusion

Conclusion

Answer the research question using the processed data, including the uncertainties.

Scientific context

Compare your findings with published values or studies (at least 3 sources), cited so they can be traced.

Evaluation

Limitations

Explain the relative impact of specific weaknesses: control of variables, precision of measurement, variation in the data, and limits to the scope of the conclusion. Distinguish random and systematic errors.

Improvements

Realistic improvements linked to each weakness, explaining how they would strengthen the conclusion.

Reference list

One consistent style (e.g. Harvard): Surname, Initials. (Year) Title in italics. Place: Publisher. For journals add the journal, volume, pages and DOI; for websites add the URL and the date accessed.
