How to use these samples

Each extract is short and invented for teaching. Read the comment, then look for the same qualities in your own writing.

There is deliberately no sample reflective statement: yours must be entirely your own experience.

1. Research questions (Criterion A)

Quality Research question Comment
Weak Effects of temperature on enzymes A topic, not a question; no focus or scope (1–2).
Improved How does temperature affect the activity of amylase? Relevant and clear but only partially focused: which amylase, which temperatures, measured how? (3–4).
Strong To what extent does temperature (20–60 °C) affect the initial rate of starch hydrolysis by fungal α-amylase (Aspergillus oryzae), measured colorimetrically at pH 6.0? Relevant, clear and focused in relation to the scope of a 4,000-word essay; "to what extent" invites analysis (5–6).
Strong (secondary data) To what extent do published field studies support the hypothesis that mangrove loss on the Kenyan coast reduces juvenile fish abundance? Focused on a defined body of evidence and a place; answerable by analysing selected studies with stated criteria.

2. Introduction extract (Criteria A and B)

"Amylases break down starch into sugars and are used in brewing and baking, where processing temperatures vary. Fungal α-amylase from Aspergillus oryzae is reported to be less heat-stable than bacterial amylases (cite). This essay investigates how the initial rate of starch hydrolysis changes between 20 and 60 °C, measured by the fall in absorbance of a starch–iodine complex, to identify the temperature range in which the enzyme is most effective."

Comment

Gives context and relevance, introduces terminology accurately (α-amylase, hydrolysis, absorbance), states the method briefly and signposts the aim. A citation supports the factual claim.

3. Descriptive versus analytical (Criterion C)

Type Extract Comment
Descriptive "At 20 °C the absorbance fell by 0.12. At 30 °C it fell by 0.25. At 40 °C it fell by 0.41. At 50 °C it fell by 0.30." Reports numbers without saying what they show or linking them to the question.
Analytical "The fall in absorbance more than tripled between 20 and 40 °C, consistent with increased collision frequency, but dropped by about a quarter by 50 °C. Because the 50 °C rate was already falling within the first minute (Figure 3), the decrease is better explained by progressive denaturation than by a lower initial activity, which supports an optimum close to 40 °C for this enzyme." Interprets the data, compares values, uses evidence from a figure, and builds the line of argument towards the conclusion.

4. Discussion and evaluation (Criterion D)

"These findings agree with the lower thermal stability reported for fungal amylases (cite), but the optimum found here is about 5 °C lower than one published value (cite), possibly because that study used a longer reaction time. The main limitation of this essay is that each temperature was tested with a single enzyme batch, so batch-to-batch variation could not be assessed; the conclusion therefore applies to this preparation rather than to the enzyme in general."

Comment

A balanced discussion of the significance of the findings, supported by evidence and sources, with an explained limitation of the essay itself: this matches the 7–8 descriptors.

5. Reflection: descriptive versus evaluative (Criterion E)

These two sentences show the difference in style only. Your statement must describe your own experience.

Type Sentence Comment
Descriptive "First I chose my topic, then I did my experiments and then I wrote my essay." Describes the process; no evaluation, examples or transfer (1).
Evaluative "When my first colorimeter readings drifted, I learned to run a blank before every set, a habit I now use in my chemistry IA because it showed me how much a result depends on controlling the instrument." Specific example, explains why it mattered, and shows transfer of learning to another context (towards 4).
