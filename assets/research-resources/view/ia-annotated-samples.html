1. Research questions (Research design)

Quality Research question Comment
Weak Does temperature affect enzymes? Stated without context: no organism, no range, no measurement. Fits the "stated without context" descriptor (1–2).
Better How does temperature affect the rate of reaction of catalase from potato? Outlined in a broad context: names the enzyme source but not the range or how the rate is measured (3–4).
Strong How does temperature (20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 °C) affect the volume of oxygen released in 60 s (cm³) by catalase from potato (Solanum tuberosum) at pH 7.0? Described in a specific context: IV with range and units, DV with unit and method, source and a controlled condition (5–6, with relevant background theory).

Teacher comment

Clear and precise: one table, units and uncertainty in the headings, consistent decimal places, means rounded to match the precision. A sample calculation of the mean and SD should follow the table. Uncertainty is considered (instrument ± 0.5 cm³ and the spread of trials).

Teacher comment on the graph

Strengths: title, axis titles with units, error bars explained in the caption, figure number. The points are joined because temperature is continuous.

Improvement: the 50 °C error bar is larger than the others; the report should comment on it (more variation as the enzyme begins to denature).

3. Statistical interpretation (Data analysis and Conclusion)

"A one-way ANOVA comparing the five temperatures gave F(4, 20) = 507.6, p < 0.001, so the null hypothesis that temperature has no effect on the volume of oxygen released is rejected at the 5% significance level. Welch's t-test showed that the mean at 40 °C (14.7 ± 0.4 cm³) was significantly higher than at 50 °C (10.4 ± 0.7 cm³), t = 12.1, p < 0.001."

Teacher comment

Good: names the test, gives the statistic, degrees of freedom and p-value, and states the decision about the null hypothesis. It reports means with their spread.

Remember: significance shows the difference is unlikely to be chance; it does not prove the mechanism. ANOVA says that at least one temperature differs; the follow-up t-test shows which (a post-hoc test is better when you compare many pairs).

4. Conclusion extracts

Level Extract Comment
Lower "The results prove that catalase works best at 40 °C." "Prove" overclaims; no numbers or uncertainties; no scientific context (1–2).
Higher "The volume of oxygen rose from 6.6 ± 0.3 cm³ at 20 °C to a peak of 14.7 ± 0.4 cm³ at 40 °C, then fell to 3.1 ± 0.3 cm³ at 60 °C. The rise is consistent with greater kinetic energy increasing enzyme–substrate collisions; the fall above 40 °C suggests denaturation of the active site. A peak between 40 and 50 °C agrees with values reported for potato catalase in published studies (cite them), although the 10 °C interval means the true optimum could lie anywhere in that range." Answers the question with processed numbers and uncertainties, explains the biology, compares with cited context and notes the limit of the interval (5–6).

5. Evaluation table

Weakness (specific) Effect on results Improvement and why
Temperature in the boiling tube fell by up to 2 °C while the syringe was attached (systematic error). Actual temperatures were lower than stated, so the rates at 50 and 60 °C may be overestimated; the peak position is uncertain. Keep the reaction tube in the water bath for the whole minute and record the temperature with a digital thermometer (± 0.1 °C), so the IV is controlled.
Potato discs came from different potatoes (biological variability). Catalase concentration varies between tubers, increasing the spread (SD up to 0.7 cm³). Use discs cut from one potato with a cork borer of fixed diameter and thickness, or use a standardised catalase solution.
Only five temperatures, 10 °C apart (scope). The optimum can only be located to within a 20 °C range. Add 35, 45 and 55 °C to locate the optimum more precisely.

Teacher comment

Specific weaknesses with their direction and relative impact explained, linked to realistic improvements: this matches the 5–6 descriptors. "Human error" or "more repeats" without detail would only be generic (1–2).
