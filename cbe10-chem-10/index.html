1.5 Trends across a period and uses

Kenya CBE Senior School · Grade 10 Chemistry · 1.5 Trends across a period and uses · Sub-strand 1.5 · Grade 10 · 60 minutes · Prepared by VistaEd
Guiding question: How do the properties of elements change from left to right across Period 3?

🎯 Start here

Walk across Period 3 from sodium to argon and you pass a soft reactive metal, a light alloy metal used in aircraft, the semiconductor in every phone chip, the phosphorus in fertilisers, the sulfur of volcanic vents in the Rift Valley and Indonesia, chlorine for water treatment and an inert gas. Why do properties change so much across one row?

STEP 1 · QUESTION

Question: same shell, different behaviour

0 – 10 min
  • Period 3: Na, Mg, Al, Si, P, S, Cl, Ar. All have electrons in three shells.
  • Across the period the number of protons (nuclear charge) increases by one each step, and one more electron is added to the same outer shell.

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Explore: radius and ionisation energy

10 – 22 min
  • Atomic radius decreases across the period: greater nuclear charge pulls the same number of shells closer.
  • First ionisation energy (energy to remove one electron from each atom in a mole of gaseous atoms) generally increases: electrons are held more tightly.
  • Electronegativity increases: atoms attract shared electrons more strongly.

STEP 3 · TEST

Test: melting point and conductivity

22 – 35 min
  • Na, Mg, Al: giant metallic; melting point rises (more delocalised electrons, smaller ions); conduct electricity.
  • Si: giant covalent; highest melting point in the period; semiconductor.
  • P₄, S₈, Cl₂: simple molecular; low melting points; do not conduct.
  • Ar: monatomic; lowest melting point.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Explain: oxides, chlorides and uses

35 – 48 min
  • Oxides: Na₂O and MgO are basic (react with acids; Na₂O dissolves to form an alkali); Al₂O₃ is amphoteric (reacts with both acids and alkalis); SiO₂ is acidic (insoluble, reacts with hot alkali); P₄O₁₀ and SO₂/SO₃ dissolve to form acids.
  • Chlorides: NaCl and MgCl₂ ionic (neutral or slightly acidic solutions); AlCl₃, SiCl₄, PCl₅ covalent and react with water (hydrolysis) giving acidic solutions with fumes of HCl.
  • Uses: sodium (street lamps, coolant); magnesium (alloys, flares); aluminium (window frames, cables, cans, aircraft); silicon (computer chips, solar cells); phosphorus (fertilisers, matches); sulfur (sulfuric acid, vulcanising rubber, fungicides); chlorine (water treatment, PVC); argon (bulbs, welding).
