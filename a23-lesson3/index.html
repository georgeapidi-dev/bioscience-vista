  • Viruses do not divide; they are assembled inside a host cell.
  • Every virus must:
  • Attach to a specific site on the host cell.
  • Inject or release its nucleic acid into the cytoplasm.
  • Use the host's protein-synthesis machinery to make viral proteins and copy its genome.
  • Assemble new virus particles.
  • Release them from the host cell.
  1. Attachment: tail fibres / attachment proteins bind receptors on the host surface.
  2. Injection: the viral DNA enters the cytoplasm; the capsid stays outside.
  3. Biosynthesis: host enzymes, ribosomes, nucleotides and amino acids copy the viral DNA and make viral proteins; host DNA is broken down.
  4. Assembly (maturation): capsids, tails and DNA come together as complete particles called virions.
  5. Lysis: a viral enzyme (lysozyme) breaks the cell wall; the cell bursts and releases the virions to infect more cells.
  • Phage lambda carries genes, not machinery: ribosomes, ATP, tRNA, amino acids and nucleotides all come from the host.
  • Lysis happens only after fully functional virions are made, so one cell releases about 100 new phages.
  • The cycle takes about 30–60 minutes; each new virion can infect another cell, so numbers rise exponentially.
  • The lytic cycle kills the host cell.

By the end of this lesson I will be able to

  • Outline the phases of the lytic cycle of bacteriophage lambda.
  • Explain how the virus relies on the host for energy, nutrition and protein synthesis at each phase.
  • Sequence and annotate the lytic cycle.
The big ideaIn the lytic cycle a phage attaches, injects its DNA, uses the host to replicate its DNA and make proteins, assembles new phages and bursts the cell.

Lytic and lysogenic cyclesKhan Academy · 3:54 · plays here
  • 1. ATTACHMENT: the tail fibre binds a specific receptor protein on the outer membrane of E. coli.
  • 2. INJECTION (penetration): the DNA is injected through the tail; the empty capsid stays outside. The linear DNA closes into a circle.
  • 3. REPLICATION and PROTEIN SYNTHESIS: host nucleotides, ATP and DNA polymerase-associated machinery copy the viral DNA; viral genes are transcribed and translated on host ribosomes into capsid, tail and lysis proteins.
  • 4. ASSEMBLY (maturation): capsids are filled with viral DNA and tails are attached.
  • 5. LYSIS (release): viral enzymes break down the cell wall; the cell bursts, releasing about 100 phages. The host dies.

Notes

At every phase after injection, the virus relies on the host for energy supply (ATP), nutrition (nucleotides and amino acids), protein synthesis (ribosomes, tRNA, enzymes) and other life functions. A virus is a set of instructions that the cell follows.
