STEP 1 · QUESTION

Oxidation by hydrogen removal

0 – 14 min · C1.2.7
  • Oxidation is loss of electrons; reduction is gain of electrons. Redox reactions involve both.
  • In respiration, substrates are oxidised by dehydrogenation: removal of hydrogen atoms (each with an electron).
  • NAD (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme that accepts hydrogen: NAD + 2H → reduced NAD (NADH + H⁺). It carries hydrogen and electrons to where they are used, mainly the electron transport chain.

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Glycolysis

14 – 32 min · C1.2.8
  • In the cytoplasm, a sequence of steps, each catalysed by a different enzyme:
  • 1. Phosphorylation: two phosphates from 2 ATP are added to glucose, making it less stable (6C hexose bisphosphate).
  • 2. Lysis: it splits into two 3C triose phosphates.
  • 3. Oxidation: hydrogen is removed from each triose phosphate and accepted by NAD → 2 reduced NAD.
  • 4. ATP formation: 4 ATP are made by substrate-level phosphorylation, giving 2 pyruvate (3C).
  • Net yield per glucose: 2 ATP, 2 reduced NAD, 2 pyruvate. No oxygen required. You do not need intermediate names.

STEP 3 · TEST

Lactate keeps glycolysis going

32 – 44 min · C1.2.9
  • Glycolysis needs a supply of NAD. Without oxygen, reduced NAD cannot pass its hydrogen to the electron transport chain, so NAD would run out and glycolysis would stop.
  • In human muscle, pyruvate accepts hydrogen from reduced NAD, becoming lactate and regenerating NAD.
  • This allows glycolysis to continue, with a net yield of 2 ATP per glucose.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Yeast: bread and beer

44 – 58 min · C1.2.10
  • The pathway in yeast is the same as in humans up to pyruvate. Only the regeneration of NAD differs: pyruvate is converted to ethanol and carbon dioxide, oxidising reduced NAD back to NAD.
  • Baking: CO₂ bubbles make dough rise; ethanol evaporates during baking.
  • Brewing: ethanol accumulates in the drink; CO₂ may be kept for fizz. Fermentation stops when sugar runs out or ethanol reaches toxic levels (about 15%).
