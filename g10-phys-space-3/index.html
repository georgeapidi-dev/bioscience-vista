The Big Bang theory

MYP 5 Sciences · Grade 10 · Physics · MYP 5 Space and the universe · Lesson 3 · Physics unit · lesson 3 of 3 · Grade 10 · Physics · 60 minutes · Prepared by VistaEd
Guiding question: Almost every galaxy we can see is moving away from us. What does that tell us about the past?
Virtual labsGraph and drawing tools
🎯 Start here

In the 1920s astronomers found that the light from distant galaxies was redder than it should be. In 1964 two engineers could not get rid of a faint hiss in their radio antenna, even after cleaning out the pigeons. Both observations turned out to be evidence for the same idea: the universe had a beginning.

By the end of this lesson I will be able to

  • Describe red-shift and explain how it is evidence that the universe is expanding.
  • Outline the Big Bang theory and the evidence from background radiation.
  • Evaluate how evidence supports a scientific theory and state what is still unknown.
  • A galaxy is a collection of billions of stars. The universe contains billions of galaxies.
  • When a source of waves moves away from you, the waves are stretched: their wavelength increases. You hear this when a siren drops in pitch as an ambulance drives away.
  • The same happens to light. Light from a galaxy moving away from us has a longer wavelength, so it is shifted towards the red end of the spectrum. This is red-shift.
  • Astronomers measure it using the dark lines in a galaxy's spectrum: the pattern of lines is the same as in the laboratory, but moved towards the red.
  • The bigger the red-shift, the faster the galaxy is moving away.
  • Light from almost every distant galaxy is red-shifted: nearly all galaxies are moving away from us.
  • The more distant a galaxy is, the bigger its red-shift, so the faster it is moving away.
  • This does not mean we are at the centre. Dots drawn on a balloon all move apart as it is blown up, and every dot sees every other dot moving away.
  • Space itself is stretching: the universe is expanding.
  • If everything is moving apart now, then in the past everything must have been closer together.
  • The Big Bang theory: about 13.8 billion years ago, all the matter and energy of the universe was in a very small, extremely hot and dense region.
  • It began to expand and has been expanding and cooling ever since. It was not an explosion into empty space: space itself began to expand.
  • As it cooled, particles, then atoms (mostly hydrogen and helium), then stars and galaxies formed.
  • Evidence 1: red-shift of the galaxies shows the expansion.
  • Evidence 2: cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR). Weak microwaves come from every direction in space. They are the radiation left over from the hot early universe, stretched to longer wavelengths by the expansion. The theory predicted this radiation before it was found in 1964.
  • A scientific theory is an explanation that is supported by a lot of evidence. It is not a guess.
  • An older idea, the Steady State theory, said the universe had always existed and looked the same. It could explain red-shift, but it could not explain the background radiation. So scientists accepted the Big Bang theory.
  • A theory is accepted while it explains all the evidence, and it is changed or replaced when new evidence does not fit.
  • Still unknown: what caused the Big Bang; what dark matter is; and why the expansion is speeding up (the cause has been named dark energy).
  • So the Big Bang theory is the best explanation we have, and research continues.
The big ideaThe light from distant galaxies is red-shifted, which shows the universe is expanding; this and the background radiation support the Big Bang theory.

Stretched light

10 – 25 min · Criterion A
  • A source moving away → waves stretched → longer wavelength.
  • For light, longer wavelength means shifted towards red: red-shift.
✏️ Worked example: From red-shift to expansion
  1. Spectral lines from a galaxy appear at longer wavelengths than in the lab.
  2. The galaxy is moving away from us.
  3. More distant galaxies → bigger red-shift → faster recession.
  4. So the universe is expanding: run backwards → a hot, dense beginning.
