STEP 1 · QUESTION

Forces between molecules

0 – 8 min · 3.6.3(a), 3.6.4
  • Intermolecular forces act between molecules. Van der Waals' forces is the general name for all of them.
  • They are much weaker than ionic, covalent and metallic bonding. When a molecular substance melts or boils, only the intermolecular forces are overcome: the covalent bonds inside the molecules are not broken.

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Polar bonds and polar molecules

8 – 20 min · 3.6.2
  • A bond between atoms of different electronegativity is polar: it has a δ+ end and a δ− end (a dipole).
  • A molecule is polar only if its bond dipoles do not cancel. This depends on its shape.
  • HCl, H₂O (non-linear) and NH₃ (pyramidal) are polar. CO₂ (linear), BF₃ (trigonal planar) and CCl₄ (tetrahedral) are non-polar: they are symmetrical, so the dipoles cancel.

STEP 3 · TEST

Two kinds of van der Waals' force

20 – 34 min · 3.6.3(b)
  • Instantaneous dipole–induced dipole (id–id) forces, also called London dispersion forces, act between all molecules. The electrons in a molecule are constantly moving, so at any instant one side is slightly negative. This instantaneous dipole induces a dipole in a neighbouring molecule, and the two attract.
  • id–id forces are stronger when molecules have more electrons and more surface in contact.
  • Permanent dipole–permanent dipole (pd–pd) forces act between polar molecules, as well as id–id forces: the δ+ end of one molecule attracts the δ− end of another.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Hydrogen bonding and water

34 – 48 min · 3.6.1, 3.6.3(c)
  • A hydrogen bond is a special, strong case of a permanent dipole–permanent dipole force. It forms when hydrogen is bonded to a very electronegative atom (N or O), between the δ+ hydrogen atom of one molecule and a lone pair on the N or O atom of another.
  • Hydrogen bonding explains the unusual properties of water: a relatively high melting point and boiling point, and a high surface tension.
  • Ice is less dense than liquid water: in ice each molecule is held by hydrogen bonds to four others in an open lattice, so the molecules are further apart than in the liquid.
