Notes

Volume is the amount of space an object takes up, in cubic centimetres (cm³). One cm³ is a cube with sides of 1 cm, about the size of a sugar cube. For a cuboid, V = length × width × height; for a cube every side is the same, so V = l × l × l. Watch your units: cm is a length, cm² is an area, cm³ is a volume.

Notes

Density tells you how much mass is packed into each cm³ of a substance: density = mass ÷ volume (ρ = m ÷ V), in g/cm³. A density of 2.5 g/cm³ means every cm³ has a mass of 2.5 g. Water is 1.0 g/cm³. Wood (about 0.6 g/cm³) floats however big the piece; stone (about 2.5 g/cm³) sinks however small. Being heavy is not what decides it: density is.

Notes

A stone is irregular, so no formula gives its volume. Use water displacement: the rise in the water level equals the volume of the stone. Volume of stone = final volume − initial volume. Read the bottom of the meniscus with your eye level with it, and work out what one small line is worth before you start.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Practical B: the density of a stone

40 – 62 min · Task 4 · Q1–Q4
▶ Volume by water displacementThe whole method, from the first reading to the calculation
  • 1. Measure the mass of the DRY stone first: a wet stone gives a bigger reading.
  • 2. Half fill the cylinder, stand it on the bench, and read the initial volume at eye level.
  • 3. Tie the thread round the stone and lower it gently until it is completely covered.
  • 4. Tap the cylinder gently to release any air bubbles stuck to the stone.
  • 5. Read the final volume at eye level.
  • 6. Volume = final − initial; density = mass ÷ volume.
