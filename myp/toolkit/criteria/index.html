MYP toolkit

🎯 Criteria A to D

What each criterion asks for, and how to reach the top levels.

MYP science is marked against four criteria. Each is worth the same, and each is marked in levels from 1 to 8 (0 means the work did not reach level 1). Every lesson on this site says which criteria it practises, and every question says which criterion it is marked on.

CriterionWhat it is aboutWhere you meet it on this site
A · Knowing and understandingKnowing the science, using it to solve problems, and judging informationMost lesson questions and exam-style questions
B · Inquiring and designingPlanning an investigation: the question, the hypothesis, the variables, the methodLessons with a practical or a virtual lab
C · Processing and evaluatingTables, graphs, conclusions and evaluating the methodLessons with data, the graph tool and the table tool
D · Reflecting on the impacts of scienceHow science is used to solve a real problem, and what follows from thatLessons that end with an issue to weigh up

What the higher levels ask for

The same question can be answered at different levels. Moving up means saying more about how and why, and coping with situations you have not seen before. These are the Criterion A levels for MYP 4 and 5 (Grades 9 and 10).

LevelYour scienceProblems you can solveWhat you do with information
1 to 2You state itYou suggest solutions to problems in familiar situationsYou interpret it to make judgments
3 to 4You outline itYou solve problems in familiar situationsYou interpret it to make scientifically supported judgments
5 to 6You describe itYou solve problems in familiar situations and suggest solutions in unfamiliar onesYou analyse it to make scientifically supported judgments
7 to 8You explain itYou solve problems in familiar and unfamiliar situationsYou analyse and evaluate it to make scientifically supported judgments

The top level grows with you

What earns level 7 to 8 depends on your year. Lessons on this site use the command terms for your grade.

Your gradeCriteria usedLevel 7 to 8 in Criterion A
Grade 6 (MYP 1)Year 1Outline scientific knowledge; solve problems in familiar situations and suggest solutions in unfamiliar ones; interpret information to make scientifically supported judgments
Grades 7 and 8 (MYP 2 and 3)Year 3Describe scientific knowledge; solve problems in familiar and unfamiliar situations; analyse information to make scientifically supported judgments
Grades 9 and 10 (MYP 4 and 5)Year 5Explain scientific knowledge; solve problems in familiar and unfamiliar situations; analyse and evaluate information to make scientifically supported judgments

Command terms: answer what the question asks

Command termWhat to write
State, identifyA name, a value or a short phrase. No explanation.
OutlineA brief account: the main points only.
DescribeA detailed account of what happens, step by step.
ExplainA detailed account with reasons: how and why it happens.
AnalyseBreak the information into parts and show what each part tells you.
EvaluateWeigh strengths and limitations, then give a judgment.
SuggestPropose a sensible answer to something you have not been taught directly.

Unfamiliar situations. The hardest questions describe something you have never studied. Do not panic: find the science you know that is hidden in the story, then apply it.

Criterion B: planning an investigation

  1. Research question. "How does [what I change, with units] affect [what I measure, with units]?"
  2. Hypothesis. "If… then… because…": a prediction and the science that supports it.
  3. Variables. The independent variable (what you change, and its range), the dependent variable (what you measure, with which instrument), and the control variables (what you keep the same, why, and how).
  4. Method and safety. Numbered steps clear enough for someone else to repeat. Say how much data you will collect, and name the hazards and how you will stay safe.

Criterion C: processing and evaluating

  1. Tables. Keep raw data apart from processed data (means, rates). Every column has a heading and a unit. Keep decimal places the same.
  2. Graphs. A line graph with a line of best fit for numbers that change continuously; a bar chart for categories. Title, labelled axes with units, even scales.
  3. Conclusion. Say whether the data supports the hypothesis and quote numbers from your results.
  4. Evaluation. Name real weaknesses in the method (not "human error"), say how each affected the data, and suggest a realistic improvement.

Criterion D: reflecting on the impacts of science

  1. Introduce the problem and the science used to solve it.
  2. Explain how the science works, using the correct terms.
  3. Discuss and evaluate what follows from using the science, through a factor: moral, ethical, social, economic, political, cultural or environmental. Your factor may link to other factors.
  4. Use scientific language clearly and precisely.
  5. Give a balanced judgment, and document every source you used.
