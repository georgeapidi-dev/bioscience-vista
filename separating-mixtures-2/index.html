STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Five more methods

about 12 min
  • Evaporation: heat a solution. The solvent evaporates and the solid solute is left. Gets back the solute (e.g. salt from salt water). Quick, but the crystals are small.
  • Crystallization: heat the solution until it is saturated, then let it cool slowly. Crystals grow and are filtered off. Gives bigger, purer crystals (e.g. copper sulfate, sugar).
  • Sublimation: some solids turn straight into a gas when heated (iodine, ammonium chloride, naphthalene, dry ice). The gas cools on a cold surface and turns back to solid. Separates these solids from ones that do not sublime (e.g. ammonium chloride from salt).
  • Chromatography: separates the dyes in ink or food colouring. A solvent moves up the paper and carries each dye a different distance.
  • Simple distillation: boil the solution; the steam goes into a condenser where it cools and turns back to liquid. Gets back the pure solvent (e.g. pure water from sea water).

STEP 3 · TEST

Practical: Chromatography of black pens

about 18 min
  • Black ink is often a mixture of several coloured dyes.
  • Different dyes are carried different distances by the water. The dye that dissolves best moves the furthest.
  • The start line is drawn in pencil because pencil does not dissolve and run.

You will need (real lab)
  • Strip of filter paper or coffee filter (2 cm × 12 cm)
  • 2 different water-based black felt-tip pens
  • Pencil and ruler
  • Clear cup with 1 cm of water
  • Paper clip or tape and a pencil to hang the strip
⚠ Stay safe
  • Use water only as the solvent.
  • Do not put pens or ink near your mouth.
Method (real lab)
  1. Draw a pencil line 2 cm from the bottom of the strip.
  2. Put a small, strong dot of pen 1 on the left and pen 2 on the right of the line. Label them in pencil.
  3. Hang the strip so the bottom just touches the water. The dots must be ABOVE the water.
  4. Wait 10 minutes. Watch the water rise and the colours separate.
  5. Take out the strip when the water is near the top. Let it dry.
  6. Record the colours you see for each pen. Stick the chromatogram in your book.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

How distillation works

about 10 min
  1. The solution is heated in a flask. The water boils and turns into steam (gas).
  2. The salt does NOT boil (its boiling point is very high), so it stays in the flask.
  3. The steam passes into the condenser. Cold water flows around the outside and cools the steam.
  4. The steam condenses back into liquid water, the distillate, which drips into the collecting flask.

Distillation gets back BOTH parts: the pure solvent (distillate) and the solute left in the flask.

STEP 6 · REFLECT

Fresh water from the sea

about 7 min
  • Many dry coastal places, such as the Middle East and some island and coastal towns in East Africa, turn sea water into drinking water. This is called desalination.
  • Some desalination plants use distillation. Others push water through very fine filters.
  • Desalination gives clean water, but it needs a lot of energy and money, and the very salty waste water must be returned to the sea carefully.
