Notes

In the 1640s Jan van Helmont planted a willow in a pot of dried, weighed soil. For five years he added only rainwater. The tree went from 2.3 kg to 76.7 kg. The soil went from 90.7 kg to 90.6 kg. Where did the 74 kg of tree come from? Write ONE sentence or sketch that answers this. It is not marked; you test it at Step 3.

Notes

The rate of photosynthesis is limited by whichever factor is in SHORTEST supply. Light provides energy: more light, faster rate, until something else is limiting. Carbon dioxide is a raw material: more gives a faster rate, until light or temperature limits it. Temperature controls the enzymes of photosynthesis: warm to an optimum of about 25–30 °C, then too hot and the enzymes denature.

Notes

RESPIRATION releases energy from glucose. It happens in the MITOCHONDRIA of every living cell, plant and animal, all the time. Aerobic respiration: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water. The energy is used for movement, growth and repair, and keeping warm. Photosynthesis and respiration are linked: the products of one are the raw materials of the other. Greenhouse growers add artificial light, and paraffin heaters that warm the greenhouse and release extra carbon dioxide.
