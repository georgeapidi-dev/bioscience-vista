Notes

A research question is the question your investigation will answer. Scientists write it in the same shape every time: "How does changing ____ (the thing you change) affect ____ (the thing you measure)?" A good research question can be answered by doing something and taking measurements. "Is sugar nice?" is an opinion. "Does sugar dissolve?" can only be answered yes or no.

The three kinds of variable

▶ Independent, dependent and controlled variablesListen for "the one I change" and "the one I measure"
  • The INDEPENDENT variable is the one thing you choose to change. There is only ever ONE.
  • The DEPENDENT variable is the thing you measure. It depends on what the independent variable does.
  • The CONTROLLED variables are all the other things you deliberately keep the same. There are usually several.
  • If you change two things at once you cannot tell which one made the difference, so the test is not fair.

Notes

A hypothesis is your prediction PLUS the science reason. IF I change… (independent variable), THEN this will happen to… (dependent variable), BECAUSE… (the science reason). Example: "If I use a bigger torch battery, then the bulb will be brighter, because a bigger battery pushes more electric current through the bulb." It is fine to be wrong, as long as it is testable. Useful words: particles, energy, move faster, spread out, dissolve.

Notes

A materials list says how many and how much: not "water" but "50 cm³ of water"; not "a beaker" but "three 100 cm³ beakers". Every measurement you promised needs a piece of equipment. A procedure is a numbered list of steps; each starts with a doing word (measure, pour, add, start, stop, record, repeat), and includes the numbers. Repeat every measurement at least three times and take a mean, because one reading on its own might be a mistake.

Notes

A hazard is something that could hurt somebody. A precaution is what you do so that it does not. Every hazard needs a matching precaution. Think about hot water, glass, water on the floor and food in a laboratory.
