STEP 1 · QUESTION

From fertilisation to the fetus

0 – 12 min · Registration · Activity 1

In your book, then here

Registration. One sentence: what do you already know about how a baby develops before birth? not marked
  • COPULATION transfers semen containing sperm into the vagina. The sperm swim through the cervix and uterus into the oviducts.
  • FERTILISATION happens in the OVIDUCT: one sperm enters the egg and the two nuclei fuse, giving a zygote with 46 chromosomes. The egg then changes so no other sperm can enter.
  • The zygote divides by MITOSIS as it travels to the uterus, and about six or seven days later IMPLANTS in the thickened lining.
  • GESTATION lasts about 38 weeks from fertilisation. The embryo (a FETUS from about week 9) grows entirely by mitosis, so every cell carries the same 46 chromosomes.
  • The PLACENTA develops in the uterus wall and is joined to the fetus by the UMBILICAL CORD. The fetus floats in AMNIOTIC FLUID in the amniotic sac.

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

The placenta

12 – 25 min · Activity 1 · Part A

🎬 Videos for this lesson

▶ Understanding the PlacentaHow the placenta forms and what crosses it. Watch before Q2 and Q3.
  • The placenta has thousands of finger-like VILLI: a very large surface area, and a very thin barrier between the two blood supplies, so diffusion is fast. The two bloods NEVER MIX.
  • INTO the fetus: oxygen, glucose, amino acids, water, vitamins, minerals and some antibodies. OUT of the fetus: carbon dioxide and urea.
  • The placenta also releases PROGESTERONE and OESTROGEN, which keep the uterus lining in place during pregnancy.
  • Some harmful substances also cross: alcohol, nicotine, some medicines and some viruses.
  • AMNIOTIC FLUID cushions the fetus against knocks and keeps its temperature steady.

STEP 3 · TEST

Birth and lactation

25 – 37 min · Activity 1 · Part B
▶ Labour and Vaginal Birth (animation)From the first contractions to the delivery of the placenta. Watch before Q6.
  • Birth (PARTURITION) has three stages. 1: the uterus wall contracts and the cervix dilates to about 10 cm; the amniotic sac usually breaks. 2: contractions push the baby head-first through the cervix and vagina; the cord is cut. 3: the placenta is delivered as the AFTERBIRTH.
  • Contractions are controlled by POSITIVE FEEDBACK: the head presses on the cervix → the pituitary releases OXYTOCIN → stronger contractions → the head presses harder → more oxytocin… until the baby is born.
  • Lactation: PROLACTIN makes the mammary glands produce milk; OXYTOCIN releases it when the baby suckles (another positive feedback loop).
  • The first milk, COLOSTRUM, is rich in antibodies, passing the mother's immunity to the baby.

STEP 5 · CHALLENGE

IVF: applied biology, and its impact

47 – 70 min · Activity 2 Part B · Activity 3 (Criterion D)
▶ How In Vitro Fertilisation (IVF) Works (Yale Medicine)A clear walk-through of the six steps. Watch before Q13 to Q17.
  • IVF is used when a couple cannot conceive naturally: blocked oviducts, too few sperm, or an unknown cause. "In vitro" means "in glass": fertilisation happens in a dish, not the oviduct.
  • 1 HORMONE TREATMENT: FSH-type hormones make many follicles mature at once instead of one; ultrasound tracks them.
  • 2 EGG COLLECTION with a fine needle. These eggs have completed the first division of meiosis: haploid.
  • 3 SPERM SAMPLE: washed, and the healthiest, most active sperm selected. Sperm are made by meiosis in the testes.
  • 4 FERTILISATION in a dish at body temperature, or one sperm injected into an egg (ICSI). A zygote: 46 chromosomes.
  • 5 CULTURE: the zygotes divide by mitosis for 3–5 days into balls of cells; embryologists check them.
  • 6 EMBRYO TRANSFER: usually ONE embryo is placed in the uterus, its lining prepared with progesterone. Spare embryos can be frozen.
  • Only one embryo is usually transferred because twins or triplets carry much higher risks of premature birth and low birth weight, and complications for the mother.
