Guiding question: Does a toy car go faster down a steeper ramp? How can we find out fairly?

Engineers who design roads in hilly places like the Kenyan highlands must know how slope affects speed. You can investigate the same question in class with a ramp, a toy car, a metre rule and a stopwatch.

By the end of this lesson I will be able to

  • Write a testable question and hypothesis, and identify independent, dependent and control variables.
  • Collect, tabulate and graph results, and calculate average speed.
  • Evaluate the method and suggest improvements.
  • Question: How does the height of a ramp affect the average speed of a toy car?
  • Independent variable (what I change): ramp height (10, 20, 30, 40, 50 cm).
  • Dependent variable (what I measure): time to travel 1 m along the floor → average speed = 1 ÷ time.
  • Control variables (kept the same): same car, same ramp length and surface, same starting point, release without pushing, same distance measured.
  • Hypothesis: the higher the ramp, the faster the car, because it has more gravitational potential energy to turn into kinetic energy.
  • Results table: height (cm) | time trial 1, 2, 3 (s) | mean time (s) | average speed (m/s).
  • Repeat each height three times; ignore anomalous results; calculate the mean.
  • Speed = distance ÷ mean time.
  • Plot a graph of average speed (y) against ramp height (x) on graph paper; draw a line of best fit.
  • Sources of error: reaction time when starting and stopping the stopwatch; the car swerving; measuring the height.
  • Improvements: use a longer distance, light gates or a phone slow-motion video; guide the car with rails; more heights; more repeats.
  • Did the results support the hypothesis?
The big ideaA fair test changes one variable, measures another and keeps the rest the same; repeating measurements and calculating a mean makes results more reliable.

✏️ Worked example: Average speed down a ramp
  1. Ramp length 1.2 m; times 0.82, 0.80, 0.84 s.
  2. Mean time = 2.46 ÷ 3 = 0.82 s.
  3. Average speed = 1.2 ÷ 0.82 ≈ 1.46 m/s.
  4. Repeat for each height and compare.
