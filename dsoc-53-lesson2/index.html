Guiding question: How can digital systems help us use resources more responsibly?

A company offers a 3D printing service so customers can design household goods from recycled plastic. Apps let neighbours share tools instead of each buying their own. Ride-sharing and bike-share systems change how cities use roads. Digital product passports could tell buyers where materials came from. But online shopping and fast delivery also push consumption higher. Responsible use of resources is a design challenge.

STEP 1 · QUESTION

Linear v circular economy

0 – 10 min · 5.3B
  • Linear economy: take → make → use → throw away.
  • Circular economy: design out waste; keep products and materials in use through reuse, repair, remanufacture and recycling.
  • Digital roles: tracking materials, connecting second-hand buyers and sellers, repair guides, product passports.

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Responsible production and distribution

10 – 25 min · 5.3B
  • Supply-chain traceability: tracking where materials and products come from (e.g. conflict-free minerals, deforestation-free food).
  • Digital product passports: records of a product's materials, repairability and recycling (planned in the EU for batteries and other products).
  • Demand forecasting with AI reduces overproduction and food waste.
  • But: fast online delivery, returns and targeted ads can increase consumption and emissions.
✏️ Worked example: Case: 3D printing with recycled plastic
  1. A company offers a 3D printing service for customers to design household goods using recycled plastic (an IB guide sample).
  2. Benefits: uses waste plastic, local production, customised goods, less shipping.
  3. Issues: printing energy, quality of recycled material, cost, whether it replaces or adds to consumption.
  4. Community makerspaces in many cities run similar projects.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Designing shared infrastructures

40 – 55 min · 5.3B
  • Energy: smart meters, community energy, time-of-use pricing.
  • Transport: public transport apps, bike-share, "mobility as a service" combining modes in one app, congestion charging.
  • Built spaces: building information modelling (BIM), sensors to use space efficiently, adaptable buildings.
  • Design questions: who is included, what behaviour is encouraged, who owns the data?
