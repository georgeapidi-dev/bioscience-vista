STEP 2 · EXPLORE

The four hormones, and puberty

5 – 20 min · Criterion A

🎬 Videos for this lesson

▶ Reproductive hormones (Cognito)Where the sex hormones are made and what they do. Watch before the table.
  • A HORMONE is a chemical messenger, made in a GLAND, carried in the BLOOD, that changes what a TARGET ORGAN does. Nerves are fast and short-lived; hormones are slower but last longer.
  • TESTOSTERONE (testes): male secondary sexual characteristics from puberty (deeper voice, facial and body hair, muscle growth, growth of penis and testes); keeps sperm production going.
  • OESTROGEN (ovaries): female secondary sexual characteristics (breasts, wider hips, pubic hair, start of the menstrual cycle); in every cycle it repairs and thickens the uterus lining.
  • PROGESTERONE (CORPUS LUTEUM, the yellow body left in the ovary after ovulation): keeps the uterus lining thick and well supplied with blood so an embryo can implant.
  • OXYTOCIN (pituitary gland): makes the uterus muscle contract during birth, and releases milk from the breast when a baby suckles.
  • Puberty is not one event: the hormones are released over several years, so the changes are gradual.

STEP 3 · TEST

The menstrual cycle, day by day

20 – 34 min · Criterion A
  • The MENSTRUAL CYCLE is the roughly 28-day cycle that prepares the uterus for a possible pregnancy. DAY 1 is the first day of bleeding, not ovulation.
  • Days 1–5, MENSTRUATION: the lining built up last cycle breaks down and is lost through the vagina.
  • Days 5–14: a FOLLICLE in the ovary grows and ripens one egg. Oestrogen from the follicle repairs and thickens the lining again.
  • About day 14, OVULATION: the follicle bursts and the egg is released into the oviduct, where it can be fertilised for about 24 hours.
  • Days 14–28: the empty follicle becomes the CORPUS LUTEUM and makes progesterone, holding the thick lining ready for implantation.
  • If there is no fertilisation, the corpus luteum breaks down at about day 28, progesterone falls, the lining is lost: day 1 again.
  • 28 days is an average, not a rule: cycles of 21 to 35 days are normal and vary between people and from month to month.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Reading the hormone graph

34 – 48 min · Interpreting data
▶ How the four hormones interactFSH, oestrogen, LH and progesterone, in the order they act.
  • FSH (follicle stimulating hormone, from the pituitary) is highest EARLY in the cycle, because its job is to start a follicle growing.
  • OESTROGEN comes from the growing follicle, so it RISES through the first half and peaks just before ovulation.
  • LH (luteinising hormone, pituitary) comes as a sudden SURGE that bursts the follicle, so its peak sits exactly AT ovulation.
  • PROGESTERONE comes from the corpus luteum, which only exists after ovulation, so it is low in the first half and peaks at about DAY 21.
  • Describe a curve with its shape AND its numbers: "rises from day 5, peaks at day 12, then falls sharply" scores; "goes up and down" does not.

No fertilisation: corpus luteum breaks down, progesterone falls, lining shed
  • The hormones do not act independently: each is the trigger, or the brake, for the next.
  • NEGATIVE FEEDBACK: a change causes an effect that cancels the original change. Rising oestrogen and progesterone switch FSH off, so once one follicle is developing, no more are started (like a thermostat).
  • If the egg IS fertilised, the embryo keeps the corpus luteum alive, progesterone stays high, the lining is not shed and the period is missed.
  • The combined contraceptive pill contains oestrogen and progesterone, which keep FSH switched off so no follicle matures. Fertility drugs supply FSH and LH.

Oxytocin and positive feedback

  • OXYTOCIN (pituitary) has two jobs: in BIRTH it makes the uterus wall contract; in LACTATION it lets milk down when the baby suckles. Prolactin MAKES the milk; oxytocin RELEASES it.
  • Birth runs on POSITIVE FEEDBACK: contractions push the baby's head onto the cervix, the stretching releases more oxytocin, which causes stronger contractions…
  • Negative feedback keeps something steady. Positive feedback drives a process to completion, then stops: exactly what birth needs.
