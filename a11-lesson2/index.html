STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Water as a solvent

16 min · A1.1.5

Check your answers to questions 1 and 2 against the model answers after you save step 1. Correct anything you got wrong in a different colour in your book: do not rub it out. The correction is the revision.

Notes: read this before you answer

  • Water is an excellent solvent for hydrophilic ("water-loving") substances: molecules and ions that are polar or charged, such as glucose, amino acids, and sodium and chloride ions.
  • An ion in water becomes surrounded by a hydration shell: the δ− oxygen of water turns towards a positive ion, and the δ+ hydrogens turn towards a negative ion. This pulls the ions apart and holds them in solution.
  • This matters for two reasons. Most enzymes catalyse reactions in aqueous solution, and most substances must be dissolved before they can be transported, in blood plasma, in xylem sap or in phloem sap.
  • Some molecules, such as lipids, are hydrophobic ("water-fearing") and do not dissolve. That insolubility is just as important biologically: it is what allows lipids to form the boundary of every cell membrane.

STEP 3 · TEST

Water and air compared: seal and loon

22 min · A1.1.6

Water and air have very different physical properties, and those differences shape the animals that live in each. Use the figures in this table in your answers: an answer that quotes a number is worth more than one that says "water is denser".

PropertyWaterAir
BuoyancyHighVery low
ViscosityAbout 55 × that of airLow
Thermal conductivityAbout 25 × that of airLow
Specific heat capacityAbout 4.2 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹ (high)About 1.0 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹ (low)

The two animals

  • The ringed seal (Pusa hispida) lives in Arctic waters and on sea ice. It has a thick layer of blubber under the skin, a streamlined body and limbs modified into flippers. On land it moves awkwardly.
  • The black-throated loon (Gavia arctica) dives and swims underwater to catch fish, and also flies. It traps a layer of air within its feathers.
▶ Water and air: animal adaptationsThe supporting clip from your notes page for this section.
A warning worth heeding for question 7. Thermal conductivity and specific heat capacity are different properties. Conductivity is how fast heat moves out of the seal. Capacity is how hard it is to change the temperature of the water itself. Examiners test this distinction, and most candidates confuse them.
