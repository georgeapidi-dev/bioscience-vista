STEP 1 · QUESTION

Chromosomes: where the DNA lives

about 7 min · Engage

🎬 Videos for this lesson

▶ Chromosomes and Karyotypes (Amoeba Sisters)Listen for: what a chromosome is, why humans have 46 (23 pairs), and what makes two chromosomes homologous.
  • A CHROMOSOME is one very long DNA molecule, wound round proteins called HISTONES and coiled up tightly (supercoiled), found in the nucleus.
  • A human body cell has 46 chromosomes: 23 PAIRS.
  • In each pair, one chromosome came from your mother and one from your father.
  • The two chromosomes of a pair are HOMOLOGOUS: the same length, with genes for the same characteristics in the same positions. The versions of a gene (alleles) can be different.
  • A KARYOTYPE is a picture of all the chromosomes of one cell, sorted into numbered pairs by size.

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Replication: copying the DNA

about 9 min · Explore
▶ DNA Replication (Amoeba Sisters)Listen for: when replication happens, base pairing (A–T, C–G) and why it is called semi-conservative.
  • REPLICATION copies the DNA. It happens BEFORE a cell divides, so each new cell gets a complete, identical copy.
  • The double helix unzips: the hydrogen bonds between the bases break and the two strands separate.
  • Each old strand is a TEMPLATE. Free nucleotides pair with it: A with T, and C with G (complementary base pairing).
  • The result is two DNA molecules. Each has one OLD strand and one NEW strand, so half of each molecule is conserved: SEMI-CONSERVATIVE replication.
  • The two molecules are identical to each other and to the original.

STEP 3 · TEST

Transcription and translation: from gene to protein

about 21 min · Apply

Part A · Transcription

▶ From DNA to Protein – 3D (yourgenome)Watch the first half: where the mRNA is made, which base replaces T, and why the DNA stays in the nucleus.
  • TRANSCRIPTION makes a copy of ONE gene as messenger RNA (mRNA). It happens in the NUCLEUS.
  • The DNA unzips at that gene. RNA polymerase uses one strand (the TEMPLATE strand) to build the mRNA.
  • Pairing rules: DNA A → mRNA U, DNA T → mRNA A, DNA C → mRNA G, DNA G → mRNA C. RNA uses URACIL (U) instead of thymine.
  • The DNA is the master copy and is too large to leave through the nuclear pores. The small mRNA copy leaves instead, and the DNA stays safe.
  • Only the gene for the protein needed right now is transcribed: copying everything would waste energy and materials.

Part B · Translation

▶ Protein Synthesis: Transcription and Translation (Amoeba Sisters)Listen for: ribosomes, codons, how to read a codon chart, and how a tRNA anticodon matches a codon.
  • TRANSLATION builds the protein. It happens at a RIBOSOME in the CYTOPLASM.
  • The ribosome reads the mRNA three bases at a time. Each group of three is a CODON, and each codon codes for one AMINO ACID.
  • tRNA molecules bring the amino acids. Each tRNA has an ANTICODON that pairs with the codon (A with U, C with G).
  • AUG is the START codon (methionine). A STOP codon (such as UAA) ends the chain.
  • The finished chain of amino acids is a POLYPEPTIDE, which folds up to become a protein.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Mutation: when one base goes wrong

about 12 min · Explain
▶ Mutations (Amoeba Sisters)Listen for: substitution versus insertion, what a frameshift is, and why not every mutation is harmful.
  • A MUTATION is a change in the base sequence of DNA.
  • SUBSTITUTION: one base is swapped for another. Only ONE codon changes, so at most one amino acid changes (sometimes none).
  • INSERTION: an extra base is added. DELETION: a base is lost.
  • Insertions and deletions shift the reading frame: every codon after the change is read in the wrong groups of three. This is a FRAMESHIFT, and every amino acid from that point on can change.
  • Most mutations have no effect, some are harmful, and a few are beneficial. The beneficial ones are what natural selection acts on.

STEP 5 · CHALLENGE

Putting it all together

about 11 min · Elaborate
▶ DNA, Chromosomes, Genes, and Traits (Amoeba Sisters)See the big picture: how a change in DNA can change a protein, and so change a trait.
  • The whole story: REPLICATION copies the DNA before CELL DIVISION. Each new cell then makes its proteins: TRANSCRIPTION (DNA → mRNA, nucleus), then TRANSLATION (mRNA → amino acids, ribosome).
  • A changed base in a gene is copied into the mRNA when the gene is transcribed.
  • At the ribosome, the codon containing that base may now code for a different amino acid.
  • A different amino acid can change how the polypeptide folds, so the protein may change shape and no longer do its job.
Five sentences that lose marks. “A chromosome is made of genes.” (the definition is supercoiled DNA in the nucleus) · “The DNA turns into mRNA.” (a copy is made; the DNA stays) · Writing T in an mRNA answer (RNA uses uracil) · “Replication makes two halves.” (two complete molecules, each half old and half new) · “All mutations are bad.” (most are neutral)
