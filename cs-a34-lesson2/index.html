STEP 1 · QUESTION

What a warehouse is for

0 – 10 min · A3.4.2
  • Data is copied from operational systems (sales, HR, web) by ETL (extract, transform, load) into the warehouse.
  • Subject-oriented: organised around subjects (sales, customers), not applications. Integrated: consistent formats from many sources.
  • Time-variant: keeps history with dates. Non-volatile and append-only: data is added, not changed. Optimised for queries (often denormalised).

Your answers

Ten years of monthly sales are kept
Dates from three systems converted to one format
Loaded records are never edited
Organised around "Customers" rather than "the till system"
This step: 4 marks
STEP 2 · EXPLORE

OLAP

10 – 25 min · A3.4.3
  • OLAP (online analytical processing) lets analysts look at data in many dimensions quickly: sales by product × store × month.
  • The OLAP cube supports slice (one month), dice (some products in some stores), drill down (year → month → day) and roll up.
  • OLAP answers "what happened and where?" for business intelligence dashboards.
✏️ Worked example: A sales question
  1. Manager asks: why did revenue fall in Q3?
  2. Roll up: revenue by region shows the Coast region fell.
  3. Drill down: Coast by month shows September fell.
  4. Slice by product: one product line collapsed in September: investigate its supply.

Your answers

Q2. Which OLAP operation? [3]
From yearly sales to monthly sales
From city totals to country totals
Showing only March for all products and stores
This step: 3 marks
STEP 3 · TEST

Data mining (1)

25 – 40 min · A3.4.3
  • Data mining finds patterns in large data sets automatically (links to A4 Machine learning).
  • Classification: put items into known classes (loan: approve/decline). Clustering: find natural groups (customer segments) with no labels.
  • Regression: predict a number (next month's sales).

Your answers

Q3. Which technique? [3]
Predicting house prices
Labelling emails as spam or not spam
Grouping shoppers by behaviour with no labels given
This step: 3 marks
STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Data mining (2)

40 – 55 min · A3.4.3
  • Association rules: items that occur together ("customers who buy bread also buy milk"): used for shop layout and offers.
  • Sequential patterns: events in order (buy a phone → buy a case within a week).
  • Anomaly detection: unusual records (fraudulent card use, faulty sensors).
