STEP 1 · QUESTION

Making an army

0 – 15 min · C3.2.9
  • Only a small number of B-cells can respond to a particular antigen, far too few to make enough antibody.
  • Once activated, these B-cells divide repeatedly by mitosis to form a clone of genetically identical cells (clonal selection).
  • Most differentiate into plasma cells: cells packed with rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus that secrete large quantities of the same specific antibody (thousands of molecules per second).

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Memory

15 – 32 min · C3.2.10
  • Immunity is the ability to eliminate an infectious disease from the body before it causes symptoms.
  • Some activated B-cells become memory cells, which survive for years or decades.
  • Primary response (first infection): slow (days to weeks), small antibody peak; symptoms usually develop.
  • Secondary response (reinfection): memory cells divide rapidly into plasma cells, giving a faster, larger, longer-lasting antibody response, usually before symptoms appear.

STEP 3 · TEST

How HIV spreads

32 – 44 min · C3.2.11
  • HIV (human immunodeficiency virus) is transmitted in body fluids: blood, semen, vaginal fluids and breast milk. It is not spread by casual contact, saliva, mosquitoes or sharing food.
  • Mechanisms: unprotected sexual intercourse; sharing needles among people who inject drugs; transfusion of unscreened blood; from mother to baby during pregnancy, birth or breastfeeding; needle-stick injuries in healthcare.
  • Prevention: condoms, screening blood, sterile needles, antiretroviral therapy (also prevents transmission to babies and partners), PrEP.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

From HIV to AIDS

44 – 60 min · C3.2.12
  • HIV infects and kills only certain lymphocytes: mainly helper T-cells (CD4 cells).
  • Without helper T-cells, B-cells cannot be activated, so the body’s ability to produce antibodies falls.
  • Over years the helper T-cell count falls; when it is very low the person has AIDS (acquired immune deficiency syndrome) and suffers opportunistic infections (tuberculosis, pneumonia, Kaposi sarcoma, oral thrush) that a healthy immune system would control.
  • Antiretroviral drugs keep the virus at very low levels so people with HIV can live long, healthy lives.
