STEP 1 · QUESTION

The problem: why cells stay small

0 – 6 min · Criterion A warm-up
  • Everything a cell needs (oxygen, glucose, water, mineral ions) enters through the CELL SURFACE MEMBRANE, mostly by DIFFUSION. Waste such as carbon dioxide leaves the same way.
  • The rate at which materials get in and out depends on the SURFACE AREA of the membrane. The amount the cell needs depends on its VOLUME, because that is where all the reactions happen.
  • The problem: if a cell doubles in width, its surface area goes up 4 times, but its volume goes up 8 times. Demand grows faster than supply.
  • What matters is the SURFACE AREA TO VOLUME RATIO: how much membrane there is for every unit of cytoplasm. Small cells have a large ratio; large cells have a small ratio.
  • Today a cube of pink agar jelly models a cell. When it sits in acid, the acid diffuses inwards and turns the agar colourless, so you can see how far into the "cell" a substance has got.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Ratios, the graph and the pattern

28 – 40 min · Criterion C
How to work out a surface area to volume ratio
SURFACE AREA of a cube = 6 × side × side. VOLUME = side × side × side. Divide surface area by volume and write it against 1.
Example, 2 cm cube: SA = 6 × 2 × 2 = 24 cm²; V = 8 cm³; 24 ÷ 8 = 3, so 3 : 1.
