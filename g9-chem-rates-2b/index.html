Reaction profiles and bond energies

MYP 4 Sciences · Grade 9 · Chemistry · MYP 4 Rates, energy and equilibrium · Lesson 4 · Chemistry unit · lesson 4 of 6 · Grade 9 · Chemistry · 60 minutes · Prepared by VistaEd
Guiding question: Where does the energy in a reaction actually come from?

🎯 Start here

Burning methane in a gas cooker releases energy, yet a match is needed to start it. Both facts come from bonds: energy is needed to break them and released when new ones form. A reaction profile shows the whole story in one diagram.

By the end of this lesson I will be able to

  • Interpret reaction profiles, including activation energy and overall energy change.
  • Explain energy changes in terms of breaking and making bonds, and calculate them from bond energies.
  • Energy on the y-axis; progress of reaction on the x-axis.
  • Exothermic: products lower than reactants; ΔH negative.
  • Endothermic: products higher; ΔH positive.
  • Activation energy: from the reactants up to the peak.
  • A catalyst lowers the peak, not the start or end.
  • Breaking bonds: energy in (endothermic).
  • Making bonds: energy out (exothermic).
  • ΔH = energy to break bonds − energy released making bonds.
  • Negative ΔH → exothermic.
The big ideaBreaking bonds takes in energy; making bonds releases energy; if more is released than taken in, the reaction is exothermic. A reaction profile shows reactants, products, activation energy and ΔH.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Explain: calculate ΔH

40 – 52 min · Criterion A
  • ΔH = bonds broken − bonds made. H–H 436, Cl–Cl 243, H–Cl 432 kJ/mol.
✏️ Worked example: ΔH for burning methane: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
  1. Break: 4 C–H (4 × 413 = 1652) + 2 O=O (2 × 498 = 996) = 2648 kJ.
  2. Make: 2 C=O (2 × 805 = 1610) + 4 O–H (4 × 464 = 1856) = 3466 kJ.
  3. ΔH = 2648 − 3466 = −818 kJ/mol.
  4. Negative → exothermic (the measured value is −802; bond energies are averages).
