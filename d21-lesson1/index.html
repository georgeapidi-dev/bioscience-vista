STEP 1 · QUESTION

Cells from cells

0 – 10 min · D2.1.1
  • In all living organisms, new cells are produced only by division of a pre-existing parent cell (mother cell) into two daughter cells.
  • Unicellular organisms divide to reproduce; multicellular organisms divide for growth, replacement and repair, and to make gametes.
  • Prokaryotes divide by binary fission; eukaryotes divide the nucleus (mitosis or meiosis) and then the cytoplasm (cytokinesis).

STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Splitting the cytoplasm

10 – 25 min · D2.1.2
  • Cytokinesis is the splitting of the cytoplasm of a parent cell between the daughter cells, usually after nuclear division.
  • Animal cells: a ring of contractile actin and myosin proteins just inside the plasma membrane at the equator tightens, forming a cleavage furrow that pinches the membrane together until the cell splits.
  • Plant cells: the rigid wall prevents pinching. Vesicles from the Golgi line up at the equator and fuse to form a cell plate, assembling new membrane and cell wall that grows outward until it joins the existing wall.

STEP 3 · TEST

Equal and unequal

25 – 38 min · D2.1.3
  • Cytokinesis is usually equal, but not always.
  • Each daughter must receive at least one mitochondrion (and, in plants, a plastid), because these organelles can only be made by division of pre-existing ones.
  • Oogenesis in humans: both meiotic divisions are unequal; the egg keeps almost all the cytoplasm (nutrients and organelles for the early embryo) while tiny polar bodies receive a nucleus and little cytoplasm, and degenerate.
  • Budding in yeast: a small bud grows out of the parent cell, receives a nucleus after mitosis, and pinches off as a smaller daughter cell.

STEP 4 · EXPLAIN

Mitosis or meiosis?

38 – 55 min · D2.1.4
  • The nucleus must divide before the cell, otherwise one daughter cell would have no nucleus (anucleate) and could not make proteins or divide.
  • Mitosis maintains the chromosome number and genome: two genetically identical diploid nuclei. Used for growth, repair, asexual reproduction.
  • Meiosis halves the chromosome number (diploid → haploid) and generates genetic diversity. Used to produce gametes (animals) or spores (plants, fungi) in sexual life cycles.
