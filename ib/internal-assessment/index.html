IB Diploma Biology · Internal Assessment

The scientific investigation

Your own research question, answered with your own data. This is 20% of your final grade. Read the steps in order, use the tools below, and tick the checklist before you submit.

Prepared by George Apidi

How it is marked

Four criteria, each marked out of 6. Each criterion has level descriptors; your teacher chooses the best fit, and each criterion is judged on its own.

CriterionWhat it looks forStrong work includesMaxWeight
Research designHow clearly the research question, context and method are set out so that someone else could repeat the investigation.Research question in a specific context; variables, controls and method explained well enough to reproduce.625%
Data analysisHow well the raw data is recorded, processed and presented, with uncertainties and statistics.Mean, standard deviation and uncertainty; suitable graphs with titles, labelled axes, units and error bars; a correct statistical test with its significance checked.625%

Tool: uncertainty

The rule from the template: the uncertainty is the smallest division on the instrument. Burettes and mercury thermometers are often read to half of it.

Build your report, section by section

Open each section for guidance from the IB investigation template.

1. Title and details

A catchy title and a subtitle that states the variables and organism, e.g. How does (range of independent variable, with units) affect (dependent variable, with units) in (organism, Latin name in italics)? Also give your personal code and the word count at the start. No cover or contents page is needed.

2. Introduction

State the research question in context, explain why it matters locally and globally, and say what a preliminary test showed. Give background theory from at least three sources (ideally journal articles, not websites), then write a null and an alternative hypothesis.

3. Research design

Name the independent, dependent and controlled variables. Use at least five intervals of the independent variable and at least five repeats. Explain how each control was kept, list the apparatus, and write the method in the past tense so it can be repeated. Add a risk assessment (likelihood × severity = risk) and say how you dealt with ethics (including how any plant material was sourced) and the environment (how you disposed of materials).

4. Data analysis

Give every table and graph a title. Show the raw data, then the processed data with mean, standard deviation and uncertainty. Standard deviation is only appropriate with more than five values. Add a qualitative observation (a photo or description). Choose the test that fits your question (use the test chooser below). Test significance against a table of critical values. Then say whether the null hypothesis is accepted or rejected. A test supports a hypothesis; it does not prove it.

5. Conclusion

Answer the research question using the processed data and its uncertainties. Compare your result with the accepted scientific context and cite at least three sources so that each one can be traced.

6. Evaluation

Explain how the weaknesses (control of variables, precision of measurement, variation in the data, range of the data) affected the results. Systematic errors are reduced by good apparatus and calibration; random errors by good control of variables and repeats. Suggest realistic improvements that answer each weakness.

7. References

Use one consistent citation style, such as Harvard. Include every source you used. Missing or incorrect referencing is treated as academic malpractice.
