The extended essay

Steps to follow

Step 1: Choose a focused question

Pick a topic you care about from biology (the course or beyond it). Narrow it until one experiment, database study or survey could answer it in the word limit.

Step 2: Plan the research

Choose the methods and the sources. Check the work is feasible with the time and equipment you have, and that it does not duplicate work you have submitted for the diploma.

Step 3: Collect and analyse

Record your data as you go, keep a research log and reflect on each step with your supervisor. Analyse the results against your question.

Step 4: Write the essay

Up to 4,000 words. Answer the research question directly, use a clear structure, and cite every source in one consistent style.

Step 5: Reflect

Write the 500-word reflection on how your thinking and approach changed. This is assessed as Criterion E.

Biology topic ideas

These are examples from the IB Biology guide. Your question can be different, as long as it is focused and has a clear method.

Context: factors affecting species populations

Use databases or other secondary sources, e.g. the impact of predation by red foxes on goose nesting populations.

Context: plant growth, flowering and germination

Experiments, e.g. the effect of salt concentration on germination in two plant species.

Context: factors affecting enzyme-based reactions

Experiments, e.g. the effect of processing temperature on the urease activity of soybean.

Context: microorganism growth assays

Monitor toxic chemicals in the environment, e.g. the viability of yeast in ethanol solutions.

World studies: sustainability

Environmental or economic sustainability, e.g. food security based on crop diversity.
