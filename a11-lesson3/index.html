STEP 2 · EXPLORE

Where Earth's water came from

15 min · A1.1.7

The origin: the asteroid hypothesis

  • The early Earth was extremely hot. Most scientists think it could not simply have held on to liquid water from its own formation, so the water we have today is thought to have arrived from beyond the planet.
  • The hypothesis on the syllabus is that asteroids containing water and ice collided with the young Earth, repeatedly, over a very long period of time, delivering water to its surface.

The retention: why the water stayed

  • Delivering water is not enough. It could only be retained (kept) if two conditions were met.
  • GRAVITY. Earth's gravitational field had to be strong enough to hold on to water molecules and stop them escaping into space. A small planet or moon with weak gravity loses its water, however much is delivered.
  • TEMPERATURE. Earth had to be cool enough for water vapour to condense into liquid water. Too close to the Sun and the water stays as vapour in the upper atmosphere, where it is easily stripped away and lost.
  • Earth met both conditions, and still does. That is why it has kept its oceans for billions of years.

STEP 3 · TEST

Test the habitable zone

8 min · A1.1.8

Notes: read this before you answer

  • Every known form of life depends on liquid water. It is the medium in which metabolism happens, the solvent that carries substances into and out of cells, and a reactant in reactions such as photosynthesis and hydrolysis.
  • Because of that, scientists searching for life elsewhere in the universe search for liquid water first. Water is far easier to detect from a great distance than life itself, so it is used as an indicator: a sign that life is possible.

The habitable zone, or "Goldilocks zone"

  • Around every star there is a band of orbits called the habitable zone, named after the story in which everything has to be "just right".
  • Closer to the star than this zone, it is too hot: any water boils away. Further from the star, it is too cold: water stays permanently frozen. Within the zone, temperatures allow liquid water to exist on the surface of a planet.
  • Being in the zone makes liquid water possible. It does not make it certain, and it certainly does not make life certain. A planet in the zone still needs an atmosphere, sufficient gravity, the right chemical elements, and protection from radiation.
